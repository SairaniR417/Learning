Chapter 1: The Foundations of CPR

1.1 The Origin of Pivot Points

Before CPR, traders used Floor Pivots — levels derived from the previous session’s OHLC. Floor traders (pit traders) used them as reference levels to gauge where price might turn.

But a single pivot wasn’t enough. Markets needed a zone of equilibrium — not just a point. This is where the Central Pivot Range was born, combining three levels into a dynamic band.

1.2 What is CPR?

The Central Pivot Range (CPR) is a band of three levels:

Pivot Point (P) – true equilibrium (average of H+L+C).

Top CPR (TC) – upper band, leaning bullish.

Bottom CPR (BC) – lower band, leaning bearish.

Together:

P=(High+Low+Close)3,TC=(P+High)2,BC=(P+Low)2P = \frac{(High + Low + Close)}{3} \quad,\quad TC = \frac{(P + High)}{2} \quad,\quad BC = \frac{(P + Low)}{2}

If TC = BC, CPR becomes a single line (rarest form → extreme compression).

1.3 The Auction Market Logic

Market = continuous auction between buyers and sellers.

CPR = the fair value zone where equilibrium is found.

If price stays near CPR → balance.

If price leaves CPR → imbalance → trend.

Analogy: Think of CPR as a “weighing scale.”

Above CPR = buyers tip the scale.

Below CPR = sellers dominate.

Inside CPR = both sides balanced.

1.4 Why CPR is a Magnet

Price often revisits CPR → institutions test equilibrium before moving.

If price opens far away, it gravitates back toward CPR during the day (the magnet trade).

This magnet behavior gives CPR predictive power for both intraday traders and swing traders.

Chapter 2: CPR Formulas, Construction & Interpretation

2.1 Step-by-Step Calculation

Take previous day’s OHLC:

High = 200, Low = 180, Close = 190.

P=(200+180+190)/3=190P = (200 + 180 + 190)/3 = 190 TC=(P+High)/2=(190+200)/2=195TC = (P + High)/2 = (190+200)/2 = 195 BC=(P+Low)/2=(190+180)/2=185BC = (P + Low)/2 = (190+180)/2 = 185

So: CPR Zone = 185 – 195 (Midpoint 190)

2.2 CPR Width

Width=TC−BCWidth = TC - BC

Narrow CPR = strong trend possible.

Wide CPR = sideways/range likely.

2.3 Interpreting Position of Price

Above CPR → bullish bias.

Below CPR → bearish bias.

Inside CPR → choppy/sideways bias.

2.4 Multiple Timeframe Construction

Daily CPR → for intraday trades.

Weekly CPR → for swing trades.

Monthly CPR → for long-term positioning.

Smart traders align CPRs across timeframes for confluence.

Chapter 3: Variations and Types of CPR

3.1 Narrow CPR

Strong compression, like a spring.

Market energy is stored → explosive breakout expected.

Works best in indices (NIFTY, BANKNIFTY).

3.2 Wide CPR

Market already expanded → range-bound.

Great for scalping support/resistance bounces.

3.3 Ascending CPR

Each day’s CPR is above the previous day’s.

Indicates bullish continuation.

Institutions are buying dips.

3.4 Descending CPR

Each day’s CPR below the previous.

Bearish continuation.

Institutions distributing stock.

3.5 Inside CPR

Today’s CPR is within yesterday’s CPR.

Market in consolidation mode.

Breakout setup forming.

3.6 Outside CPR

Today’s CPR engulfs yesterday’s.

Strong volatility expansion → big moves.

3.7 Marching CPR

Continuous ascending or descending CPR.

Pure trend days.

Rule: Never fight a marching CPR.

3.8 Trend Reversal CPR

CPR shifts suddenly opposite (e.g., from ascending to descending).

Early reversal sign before charts show it.

Chapter 4: CPR in Intraday Trading

4.1 The Intraday Compass

At market open, CPR tells you:

Direction bias.

Expected volatility.

Whether to trade aggressively or stay defensive.

4.2 CPR Intraday Strategies

A. Breakout Strategy

Look for narrow CPR.

If price opens above TC → go long.

If price opens below BC → go short.

Trail stop behind CPR.

B. Reversal Strategy

If price keeps rejecting TC or BC → take counter trade back into CPR.

C. Magnet Trade

If market opens far from CPR, probability is high it will revisit CPR.

Entry when reversal signs form, target = CPR zone.

D. CPR + Initial Balance (first 15-min range)

Opening range breakout + CPR bias = high-probability setup.

4.3 CPR as Dynamic Support/Resistance

CPR acts as an intraday pivot zone.

When tested, price often bounces/rejects strongly.
