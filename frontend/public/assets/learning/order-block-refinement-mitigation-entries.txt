MODULE 6: ORDER BLOCK REFINEMENT, MITIGATION & ENTRIES

The Art of Smart Money Precision Entry

🧩 6.1 Quick Recap — What is an Order Block?

An Order Block (OB) is the last opposite candle before a large impulsive displacement.

A Bullish OB → the last bearish candle before a strong move up.

A Bearish OB → the last bullish candle before a strong move down.

These zones are where Smart Money executed bulk orders, initiating large movements. When price returns to these OBs later, it often reacts sharply — because unfilled institutional orders remain there.

📊 Visual Reference: Bearish and Bullish OBs marked before large displacements.

⚙️ 6.2 What Does “Mitigation” Mean?

Mitigation means Smart Money revisiting old positions to close or rebalance their trades.

Institutions don’t enter or exit all orders at once — they scale in and scale out. So price revisits those zones to:

Fill pending institutional orders

Hedge earlier trades

Rebalance exposure

This process is called Mitigation — and it’s the reason price “respects” the same zone multiple times.

“Mitigation = the cleanup of unfinished business by Smart Money.”

🧭 6.4 The Institutional OB Logic (How It Works)

1️⃣ Smart Money identifies liquidity. 2️⃣ Executes large orders → creating displacement (BOS). 3️⃣ Leaves behind OB (the footprint). 4️⃣ Price retraces back to mitigate unfilled orders. 5️⃣ Reaction + confirmation (CHOCH/FVG) = entry trigger. 6️⃣ Trend resumes → liquidity at opposite side becomes target.

📊 Visual: BOS → OB → Mitigation → Continuation.

⚡ 6.5 The Three Pillars of a Valid Order Block

✅ 1. Liquidity Sweep Before OB

Equal highs/lows or stop-hunt happens first.

Shows manipulation phase is complete.

✅ 2. Strong Displacement Candle

The move after OB must show clear aggression (institutional intent).

✅ 3. Structure Break (BOS or CHOCH)

Confirms that the move from OB shifted market structure.

Without all three, OBs are weak or invalid.

⚙️ 6.7 Mitigation Blocks — The Re-entry Footprints

A Mitigation Block is a new OB formed when price revisits and mitigates a previous OB.

It shows Smart Money hedging or adding to positions.

Sequence:

Original OB forms → price moves away.

Price retraces → re-enters OB → forms smaller internal OB.

This smaller internal OB = Mitigation Block.

It’s like Smart Money saying:

“We’ve rebalanced here — now continuation is safe.”

📊 Visual: Original OB → Mitigation → Internal OB.

⚡ 6.9 Breaker Blocks — The Inverted Order Blocks

A Breaker Block occurs when an existing OB fails to hold and price closes beyond it.

That invalidated OB then flips its role:

Old bullish OB becomes new bearish breaker block.

Old bearish OB becomes new bullish breaker block.

They often mark trend reversal zones.

📊 Visual: Breaker Block Role Flip.

🧭 6.10 How to Trade Using Refined OBs

🔹 Step 1: Identify HTF OB

Look for strong displacement and structure break.

Mark the last opposite candle before move.

🔹 Step 2: Drop to LTF for Refinement

Find internal OB inside that HTF zone.

Check for imbalance, liquidity, and CHOCH confirmation.

🔹 Step 3: Wait for Mitigation

Price revisits OB → forms internal reaction.

Enter on confirmation (displacement or CHOCH on lower TF).

🔹 Step 4: Manage Risk

Stop-loss just below/above OB.

Target next external liquidity or FVG.

📊 Visual: HTF → LTF Refinement Trade Flow.

This is the institutional execution model:

HTF = bias, LTF = entry.
