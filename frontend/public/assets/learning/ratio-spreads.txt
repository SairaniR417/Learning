RATIO SPREAD

A Ratio Spread is an advanced directional options strategy where the trader:

Buys 1 option,

Sells more than 1 option at another strike,

Creating a ratio (like 1:2 or 1:3).

It gives limited risk on one side but unlimited risk on the other side (depending on type).

Used when you want to capture direction + controlled premium income.

Let’s break it the right way 👇

1️⃣ What is a Ratio Spread?

A Ratio Spread is created when:

You BUY 1 option

You SELL 2 or more options of another strike

Like:

Buy 1 ATM

Sell 2 OTM

Example: Buy 22,000 CE Sell 2× 22,200 CE

This creates a 1:2 Call Ratio Spread.

Same logic applies for Puts.

2️⃣ Why is it called a Ratio Spread?

Because the number of buy vs sell options is in a ratio. Example:

Buy 1, sell 2 = 1:2 ratio

Buy 1, sell 3 = 1:3 ratio

The “spread” comes from using different strikes.

3️⃣ Why do traders use Ratio Spread?

To get:

Directional exposure

Reduced cost or ZERO cost trade

Theta advantage (because more options sold)

Volatility edge (favors IV drop)

High reward if market moves to target, NOT beyond

Ratio spread is like:

“I want direction, but I don’t want to pay full premium.”

4️⃣ When should you create a Ratio Spread?

✔ 1. IV High → Expected to Fall

Because you SALE more options → you want IV to cool.

✔ 2. When you know the TARGET zone

Ratio spread profits the MOST when price moves towards your sold strike.

✔ 3. When market moves slowly

Fast moves beyond the sold strikes can hurt.

✔ 4. When breakout direction is clear

You still need direction, but controlled direction.

5️⃣ Types of Ratio Spreads

There are two clean types:

🟩 Bull Call Ratio Spread (Bullish)

Buy 1 ITM / ATM Call

Sell 2 OTM Calls

🟥 Bear Put Ratio Spread (Bearish)

Buy 1 ITM / ATM Put

Sell 2 OTM Puts

Let me explain each separately.

🟩 TYPE 1: BULL CALL RATIO SPREAD

Bullish strategy with reduced cost, best when upside is limited.

✔ What is it?

You expect market to move up slowly. But you don’t want to pay full premium.

So you:

Buy 1 ATM/ITM Call

Sell 2 OTM Calls (where you think price will stall)

✔ Why use it?

Because:

Very cheap (sometimes zero-cost)

Theta positive (you sold more)

Profits if market goes up to a target

Limited loss on downside

High reward if market moves slowly upward

✔ When to use?

IV high

Clear bullish structure

Price expected to reach a zone

Slow uptrend

OI wall above (market may slow down there)

✔ How to create a Bull Call Ratio Spread?

Example: NIFTY 22,000

Buy 22,000 CE

Sell 2× 22,200 CE

This gives a 1:2 Call Ratio Spread → very low debit or even credit.

🟥 TYPE 2: BEAR PUT RATIO SPREAD

Bearish version: profit from slow down move.

✔ What is it?

You expect the market to fall slowly. So you:

Buy 1 ATM/ITM Put

Sell 2 OTM Puts (your target zone)

✔ Why use it?

Low cost

Theta benefit

Works when price moves down slowly

Works when IV falls after spike

✔ When to use?

Breakdown confirmed

IV high

ATR low

Market expected to fall & then slow down

Clean support target identified

✔ How to create a Bear Put Ratio Spread?

Example:

Buy 22,000 PE

Sell 2× 21,800 PE

6️⃣ Risk & Reward in Ratio Spread (Very Important)

✔ Limited Risk (if price moves as expected)

Risk is defined if price stays between long and short strikes.

✔ Unlimited Risk (if price moves beyond the sold strike too far)

Example: If you sell 22,200 CE × 2 and market jumps to 22,600 — your risk increases sharply.

This is why Ratio Spread is for controlled moves, NOT violent ones.

7️⃣ What to check before entering a Ratio Spread?

✔ IV

High IV is best. You sell more → want IV to fall.

✔ OI Walls

Sell where there is big OI, market slows here.

✔ Delta Behavior

Buy leg around Delta 0.50

Sold legs around Delta 0.20

✔ Trend

Directional bias needed.

✔ Target Level

Your sold strike must be your expected stop point for the move.

8️⃣ Advantages of Ratio Spread

Very cheap or zero-cost

Limited risk inside zone

Big profit if price moves slowly to target

High Theta benefit

Perfect for calm directional markets

Good for expiry where price gravitates to strikes

9️⃣ Disadvantages / When NOT to use Ratio Spread

❌ Trending days

Price may blast beyond sold strikes.

❌ Event days

Ratio = disaster in event spikes.

❌ When you don’t know target

If price overshoots → risk increases exponentially.

❌ When IV is low

Selling more options in low IV = no edge.

🔟 Example – Perfect Bull Call Ratio Spread Setup

NIFTY @ 22,000 Breakout confirmed Target: 22,200 (big OI wall) IV high → falling Delta drift visible Slow uptrend

Create:

Buy 22,000 CE

Sell 2× 22,200 CE

Cheap, high-probability trade.

🧘 Essence of Ratio Spread

Ratio Spread = Low cost + Direction + Target zone. Buy 1, sell 2 when IV is high and move is controlled.

BACKSPREAD (REVERSE RATIO SPREAD)

“Explosive Move Strategy — Unlimited Profit, Limited Risk.”

This is the opposite of a normal Ratio Spread. Instead of selling more options, here you BUY more options than you sell.

Used when you expect a BIG, FAST, EXPLOSIVE MOVE in one direction.

1️⃣ What is a Backspread?

A Backspread is a strategy where:

You SELL 1 option

You BUY 2 or more options at another strike

So the ratio becomes 1 : 2 (or 1 : 3) but BUYING dominates.

Example (Call Backspread):

Sell 1 ATM CE

Buy 2 OTM CE

Example (Put Backspread):

Sell 1 ATM PE

Buy 2 OTM PE

This creates long Gamma, long Vega, and explosive payoff.

2️⃣ Why is it called a “Reverse Ratio Spread”?

Because normal ratio spread = Buy 1, Sell 2 Backspread = Sell 1, Buy 2

The ratio is reversed → Hence, Reverse Ratio Spread.

3️⃣ Why do traders use a Backspread?

For two Important Reasons:

✔ 1. To capture a violent breakout/breakdown

When price moves very fast in your direction → Backspread explodes in profit (unlimited upside).

✔ 2. Risk becomes limited or zero-cost

Because you SELL ATM and BUY OTM, sometimes the entire structure becomes zero debit.

4️⃣ When should you create a Backspread?

This is VERY important.

Backspread is only for blast moves.

Use it when:

✔ IV Low → Expected to EXPLODE

This is a long Vega strategy. Best before:

Major news

Breakouts

Breakdowns

Events (Budget, Fed, RBI, NIFTY expiry after compression)

✔ Strong Trend Structure

CHoCH

BOS

Higher High / Lower Low

Imbalance breakout

✔ Delta Drift Visible

ATM Delta moves rapidly:

0.48 → 0.52 → 0.58 → 0.65 Trend is gaining strength.

✔ OI Unwind on opposite side

Call unwinding → Bullish Backspread

Put unwinding → Bearish Backspread

This confirms explosive move.

✔ Gamma Expansion (volatility spike expected)

Backspreads LOVE Gamma.

5️⃣ Types of Backspreads

There are two:

Call Backspread → Used when expecting upside explosion

Put Backspread → Used when expecting downside crash

Let’s break them clearly.

🟦 TYPE 1: CALL BACKSPREAD (Bullish Explosion Strategy)

✔ What is it?

Directional bullish strategy with unlimited upside profit.

✔ How to create a Call Backspread?

Example: NIFTY at 22,000

Sell 1 ATM CE

Buy 2 OTM CE

Example: Sell 22,000 CE Buy 2× 22,200 CE

✔ Why this works?

ATM premium is high

OTM options cheap

If breakout happens → both OTMs explode

Sell ATM reduces your cost

Buy 2 OTM gives unlimited upside

✔ When to use Call Backspread?

Big gap-up expected

Strong breakout area

IV likely to rise

Event approaching

Short covering visible

High Call unwinding

🟥 TYPE 2: PUT BACKSPREAD (Bearish Crash Strategy)

✔ What is it?

Directional bearish strategy with unlimited profit on downside.

✔ How to create a Put Backspread?

Example: Sell 22,000 PE Buy 2× 21,800 PE

✔ When to use Put Backspread?

Breakdown about to happen

IV low & rising

Long unwinding visible

Aggressive selling

Market entering liquidity pocket

Crash-like movement possible

6️⃣ Risk & Reward Logic

✔ PROFIT

Unlimited in the direction of the move

Maximum profit when breakout is VIOLENT

✔ LOSS

Limited on opposite side

If price stays between long and short strikes

Or if IV does NOT expand

✔ Break-even is F A R (good)

Backspreads have wide break-even zones.

7️⃣ What to check before entering a Backspread?

✔ IV

Must be LOW → going to rise.

✔ Gamma

Needs to be HIGH → moves explode.

✔ OI

Opposite side unwinding.

✔ Delta

Rapid drift.

✔ Price Structure

No consolidation. Must expect VOLATILE MOVE.

8️⃣ Advantages of Backspread

Unlimited profit potential

Low or zero cost

Much safer than naked buying

Great for events

High Vega exposure

Superb when volatility explodes

9️⃣ Disadvantages / When NOT To Use

❌ Not for range

Backspread loses in sideways market.

❌ Not for low ATR

Needs movement.

❌ Not for expiry (Gamma risk too high)

Only use early week/month.

❌ Avoid in high IV

You need IV to expand.
