The Complete Open Interest (OI)

Reading Institutional Intentions through OI, IV & Price Flow

🧩 1️⃣ What is Open Interest (OI)?

Definition:

Open Interest (OI) is the total number of open option or futures contracts that are currently active and not yet settled, exercised, or closed.

Each time a new buyer and a new seller create a contract → OI increases by 1. Each time either side closes their existing position → OI decreases by 1.

So OI doesn’t measure trades done today (that’s Volume). OI measures positions still held after trading ends.

🔍 Think of it like this:

Volume is activity.

OI is commitment.

A market can have high volume but low OI → traders are just scalping, no real positioning. A market with rising OI → smart money is building exposure.

🧠 Simple analogy:

“OI is like counting how many soldiers are still on the battlefield after the day’s fight ends.”

🧭 2️⃣ How OI Works Mechanically

Let’s break it down step by step:

Transaction Type

What Happens

Effect on OI

New buyer + new seller open position

New contract created

OI increases

Existing buyer sells to close, existing seller buys to close

Contract removed

OI decreases

Buyer opens new, seller closes existing

Net same

OI unchanged

Seller opens new, buyer closes existing

Net same

OI unchanged

So OI only changes when a brand new contract comes into existence or an old one disappears.

🧮 Formula (simplified)

[ OI_{today} = OI_{yesterday} + NewContracts - ClosedContracts ]

🧩 12️⃣ Advanced Tip – The OI Trap

OI is powerful — but incomplete without volume and IV. Sometimes, writers add OI to trap breakout traders (fake buildup).

So confirm:

Price closing above OI zone + rising IV → true breakout.

Price breaks zone but IV flat/falling → fake move (short cover only).

🧠 In One Sentence

“OI tells you who is in the trade, IV tells you how they feel, and Delta tells you how much conviction they have.”

🔑 As a Buyer:

Focus on falling OI + rising IV (short covering).

Focus on rising OI + rising IV (fresh long buildup).

Avoid high OI + falling IV → that’s where premium dies.

🔒 As a Seller:

Prefer rising OI + falling IV (calm range).

Avoid rising IV or collapsing OI (risk zone).
