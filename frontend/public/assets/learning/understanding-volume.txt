DELTA TO DHARMA

The Volume Bible

1. What is Volume?

Definition: Volume is the number of contracts (in derivatives) or shares (in equities) traded during a specific time period (1-min, 5-min, daily, etc.).

Why Important? Price shows “where” the market is moving, but volume shows “how strong” that move is.

Think of price as the vehicle and volume as the fuel.

👉 Example: If a stock rises ₹10 in price but with very low volume → it’s like a car moving uphill with little fuel — may not sustain. If it rises ₹10 with very high volume → it has momentum and stronger conviction.

2. Volume in Charts

Usually plotted as vertical bars under price candles.

Each bar = total contracts/shares traded in that candle’s duration.

Volume color coding (common platforms):

Green bar: More trades happened on up-ticks (price closed higher than open).

Red bar: More trades happened on down-ticks (price closed lower than open).

Neutral/Gray bar: Mixed or platform default.

⚠️ Important:

Volume bar color ≠ candle color.

A green candle can sometimes show red volume (depending on tick-based calculation).

But generally:

Green candle + Green volume = bullish confirmation.

Red candle + Red volume = bearish confirmation.

Candle vs. volume mismatch = caution, may signal exhaustion or trap.

3. Relationship Between Candles & Volumes

Price Rising + Rising Volume = Strong bullish momentum.

Price Rising + Falling Volume = Weak rally, possible exhaustion.

Price Falling + Rising Volume = Strong bearish momentum (distribution/selling pressure).

Price Falling + Falling Volume = Weak decline, possible pullback/reversal.

📌 Golden rule: Volume must expand in the direction of trend for the trend to sustain.

4. Momentum & Volume

Rising Momentum + Rising Volume: Healthy trend, strong continuation.

Rising Momentum + Dropping Volume: Trend losing steam, may reverse or consolidate.

Flat/Sideways Price + Rising Volume: Market is absorbing supply/demand, possible accumulation/distribution zone.

Breakout Candles + High Volume: Valid breakout.

Breakout Candles + Low Volume: Fake breakout / false move trap.

5. Volume in Breakouts

True Breakout: Large green candle + much higher-than-average volume (at least 1.5x – 2x average volume of past 20 bars).

False Breakout: Price crosses support/resistance but with thin volume — easy for smart money to trap retail.

Retest: After breakout, price comes back to test old level. Volume usually declines on retest → then rises again on continuation.

👉 Rule of thumb: “No volume = No trust in breakout.”

6. Volume in Bases & Consolidation

Low, drying volume: Market is waiting, no strong buyer/seller → base building.

Sudden spike in volume inside consolidation: Smart money accumulating/distributing quietly.

Tight range + Rising volume near support/resistance: Explosion likely coming.

Pro Trick: Volume inside bases gives a clue of who is controlling (buyers or sellers).

7. Volume in Support & Resistance

At support:

Price bounces with high volume → demand strong.

Price breaks support with high volume → strong breakdown.

At resistance:

Price rejects with high volume → supply dominant.

Price breaks resistance with high volume → breakout confirmed.

8. Volume in Consolidation

Healthy consolidation: Volume decreases gradually.

Accumulation phase: Flat price, but volume rises slowly → big players building positions.

Distribution phase: Flat price, but heavy volume spikes with upper wicks → smart money unloading before fall.

👉 Wyckoff Method (Advanced) relies heavily on this volume–price relationship.

9. Volume in Options

Options behave differently because volume shows trading activity in a particular strike price, not the underlying.

Volume in options = liquidity + interest.

High volume + High OI (Open Interest) = strong activity, reliable levels.

High volume but no OI rise = intraday speculation.

OI rising + Volume rising = trend building.

OI falling + Volume rising = short covering / long unwinding.

👉 Example:

If NIFTY 18000 CE has huge volume + rising OI → strong resistance.

If NIFTY 18000 PE has huge volume + rising OI → strong support.

10. Advanced Volume Tools

Volume Moving Average – Compare current bar with average (20-bar average volume).

Volume Profile – Plots volume by price levels, shows strong support/resistance zones.

On-Balance Volume (OBV) – Indicator to track cumulative money flow.

VWAP + Volume – Confirms intraday institutional buying/selling.

Accumulation/Distribution (A/D) Line – Measures flow of money into or out of an asset.

11. Pro-Level Concepts

Climactic Volume: Extremely high volume after a big trend → signals trend exhaustion (either top or bottom).

Volume Divergence: Price makes higher highs but volume doesn’t → weak rally, possible reversal.

Spring/Shakeout (Wyckoff): Price dips below support on high volume, quickly recovers → strong bullish signal.

Effort vs. Result: High volume but little price movement = absorption; smart money is absorbing supply/demand.

12. Quick Checklist for Reading Volume

✅ Is volume confirming the trend? ✅ Is breakout supported by above-average volume? ✅ Is consolidation drying volume or hidden accumulation? ✅ Are supports/resistances respected with strong volume? ✅ Is momentum matching with volume?

13. Mindset for Traders Using Volume

Never trust price without checking volume.

Volume is the voice of smart money.

Beginners often only see candles. Pros listen to the noise behind candles = volume.
