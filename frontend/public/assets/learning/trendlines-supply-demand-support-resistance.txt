DELTA TO DHARMA

Trendlines, Supply-Demand, Support & Resistance

Introduction

Every chart tells a story. But without the right tools, it’s just noise. Three of the most powerful tools traders use to decode price action are:

Trendlines – showing direction.

Support & Resistance – showing barriers.

Supply & Demand – showing institutional footprints.

Mastering these gives you the foundation to understand where price wants to move and how big players are positioned.

1. Trendlines – The Market’s Guiding Rails

Definition: A straight line connecting at least two price points, extended into the future to act as dynamic support/resistance.

Uptrend Line: Connects higher lows → shows buyers defending.

Downtrend Line: Connects lower highs → shows sellers controlling.

🔹 Key Rules

At least 2 touches; 3rd touch confirms validity.

Wick-to-wick consistency is better than mixing with bodies.

More touches = stronger reliability.

🔹 Advanced Concepts

Channels: Parallel trendlines create rising/falling channels.

Fake Breakouts: Price often pierces trendlines to trap traders before continuing.

Confluence: A trendline aligning with support/resistance or demand zone = high probability.

👉 Think of trendlines as the “roads” along which price prefers to travel.

2. Support & Resistance – The Market’s Invisible Walls

Support: A level where buyers historically defend (floor).

Resistance: A level where sellers historically block (ceiling).

🔹 Types of S&R

Horizontal: Fixed zones (e.g., 22,000 on NIFTY).

Dynamic: Moving averages, VWAP, or trendlines.

Psychological: Round numbers (100, 500, 1000 in stocks; 20,000 in NIFTY).

🔹 Rules of Thumb

Stronger on higher timeframes.

Once broken, support → becomes resistance and vice versa (role reversal).

Zones with high volume carry greater weight.

🔹 Pro Insights

Liquidity Traps: Big players know retail stops lie just beyond obvious S/R. They trigger false breaks to accumulate or distribute.

👉 S&R are like “doors” in the market – price either bounces or breaks through them.

3. Supply & Demand – The Institutional Blueprint

This concept refines S&R by focusing on where institutions placed big orders.

Supply Zone: Region where strong selling started (rally paused → sharp drop).

Demand Zone: Region where strong buying started (drop paused → sharp rally).

🔹 Patterns

Rally–Base–Drop (Supply).

Drop–Base–Rally (Demand).

🔹 Rules

Fresh Zones: First test is strongest.

Retested Zones: Lose strength over time.

Refinement: Mark on higher TF, execute on lower TF.

🔹 Identifying Clues

Large imbalance candles (big body, small wick).

Sudden volume spikes confirming institution entry.

👉 Supply & Demand show the footprints of smart money – where price was “engineered” to move.

4. Blending the Three Concepts

Trendlines = Direction.

Support/Resistance = Barriers.

Supply/Demand = Fuel zones.

Example:

A demand zone that aligns with a trendline and sits at support → triple confirmation for a long trade.

A supply zone sitting near resistance in a downtrend → high-probability short.

👉 The best trades happen where all three concepts overlap.

5. Practical Applications

Breakout Trading: Price smashing through resistance with volume = strong momentum entry.

Bounce Trading: Price holding at trendline + demand zone = reversal entry.

Trap Trading: Price faking a breakout beyond resistance, then reversing down.

Supply-Demand Flip: Demand zone turning to supply after breakdown.

6. Mistakes to Avoid

Drawing too many lines → messy charts.

Ignoring timeframe hierarchy.

Trusting one level forever (markets evolve).

Forgetting volume confirmation.
