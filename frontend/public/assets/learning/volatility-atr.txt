The ATR (Average True Range)

Chapter 1: The Foundations of ATR

1.1 What is ATR?

ATR = Average True Range, introduced by Welles Wilder (same creator of RSI, ADX, and Parabolic SAR).

Purpose: Measure volatility (not direction, not trend).

Range: 0 → infinity (no fixed scale).

Key Idea:

High ATR = High volatility.

Low ATR = Low volatility (consolidation).

⚠️ ATR doesn’t tell bullish or bearish. It just tells how wide the moves are.

Chapter 2: The ATR Formula (Under the Hood)

2.1 Step 1: True Range (TR)

TR = max of:

(High – Low)

|High – Previous Close|

|Low – Previous Close|

This captures gaps and overnight moves, not just intraday range.

2.2 Step 2: Average True Range (ATR)

ATR = Wilder’s Moving Average of TR (default: 14 periods).

Chapter 3: Reading ATR

Rising ATR → volatility increasing (breakouts, trending markets).

Falling ATR → volatility shrinking (sideways, consolidation).

Low ATR zones often precede big moves.

High ATR zones often precede cool-offs or reversals.

Chapter 4: ATR Trading Strategies

4.1 ATR as a Stop-Loss Tool (Wilder’s original idea)

Place SL = Entry Price ± (x × ATR).

Example: Buy at 100, ATR = 2, SL = 100 – (2×1.5) = 97.

This adapts to volatility → wider SL in volatile markets, tighter SL in calm markets.

4.2 ATR for Position Sizing

Position size = Risk ÷ (ATR × multiplier).

Helps normalize exposure across volatile vs calm instruments.

4.3 ATR Breakout System

Buy when price breaks above resistance + ATR filter.

Example: Resistance = 120, ATR = 2 → breakout entry above 122.

4.4 ATR with Moving Averages

Use ATR to confirm breakout strength.

Rising ATR with MA crossover = strong trend.

MA crossover with falling ATR = false signal risk.

4.5 ATR in Options Trading

High ATR = expensive options (premiums rise).

Low ATR = cheap options (good for buyers, risky for sellers).

Combine with IV (Implied Volatility) for premium strategies.

Chapter 5: ATR Psychology

High ATR = Fear/Greed dominate → big swings.

Low ATR = Indecision → traders waiting for catalysts.

ATR spikes = Panic or Euphoria.

ATR drop = Comfort zone, complacency.

Chapter 6: Advanced ATR Techniques

6.1 ATR Bands

Like Bollinger Bands, but based on ATR.

Upper Band = MA + k×ATR

Lower Band = MA – k×ATR

Great for spotting breakouts & reversals.

6.2 ATR Trailing Stop (Chandelier Exit)

Stop = Highest High – (ATR × multiplier).

Protects profits by following price with volatility-based stop.

6.3 ATR + ADX Combo

ADX = trend strength.

ATR = volatility.

Together → identify Strong Trend + High Volatility = explosive moves.

6.4 ATR Multi-Timeframe

Daily ATR for swing positioning.

Hourly ATR for intraday SL.

Weekly ATR for trend context.

Chapter 7: Common Mistakes with ATR

Thinking ATR = direction → Wrong, it only measures range.

Using fixed ATR multipliers everywhere → Each instrument behaves differently.

Ignoring timeframe → ATR on weekly vs 5-min gives different meanings.

Using ATR alone without price action → Must combine with structure.

Chapter 8: Tips & Best Practices

Use ATR for dynamic stop-loss & position sizing.

Use ATR spikes to detect trend initiation or exhaustion.

Compare ATR across instruments → trade only those offering movement.

Adjust ATR period → shorter (7–10) for intraday, standard (14) for swing, longer (20–30) for position.

Chapter 9: ATR in Different Market Types

Stocks: Identify breakout days & earnings volatility.

Forex: Excellent for pip targets & SLs.

Commodities: ATR surges during geopolitical shocks (gold, oil).

Options: ATR helps judge whether premiums are justified.

Chapter 10: ATR Holy Trinity

ATR = Volatility Meter

Combine ATR with ADX = Trend + Volatility Detector

Use ATR for Risk Management = Position size + SL

⚡ Final Thought: ATR is not about direction — it’s about breathing space. It tells you how much room the market needs to move before proving you wrong. Use it as your armor for risk control and your radar for volatility explosions.

ATR – Swing & Positional Strategies

1. ATR-Based Stop Loss & Position Sizing (Core Strategy)

Why it works: Swing and positional traders cannot afford tight stop losses. ATR helps you size stops and position according to volatility.

Rule: Place Stop Loss = Entry ± (1.5 × ATR)

Position Sizing Formula: Position Size=Risk AmountATR×MultiplierPosition\ Size = \frac{Risk\ Amount}{ATR \times Multiplier}

Example: If ATR = ₹20, you buy at ₹500, SL = ₹470 (1.5 × ATR). If you are willing to risk ₹10,000, position size = 10,000 ÷ (20×1.5) = 333 shares.

Practical use: Stops don’t get hit by random noise, yet risk is controlled.

2. ATR Breakout Swing Strategy

Why it works: Stocks break out strongly when they move more than usual volatility.

Buy Rule: If today’s close > Yesterday’s High + (1.5 × ATR) → Long.

Sell Rule: If today’s close < Yesterday’s Low – (1.5 × ATR) → Short.

Stop Loss: 1.5 × ATR opposite side.

Exit: Trail with ATR or until opposite signal comes.

Best for: Swing trading in trending stocks, commodity futures, indices.

3. ATR Bands Positional Strategy

ATR Bands act like volatility envelopes.

Calculation: Upper Band = Close + (k × ATR) Lower Band = Close – (k × ATR) (k = 2 for positional, 1.5 for swing)

Buy: When price closes above upper ATR Band → enter long.

Sell: When price closes below lower ATR Band → enter short.

Exit: Midline or opposite band.

Best for: Trend following, positional traders.

4. ATR Trend Filter with Moving Average

Why it works: Avoids sideways traps by combining volatility with direction.

Use a 50 EMA + ATR filter.

Rule:

Go long only if Price > 50 EMA and ATR > ATR(50) average (means volatility is expanding).

Go short only if Price < 50 EMA and ATR expanding.

Best for: Stock swing trades and positional index futures.

5. ATR Channel Positional Strategy (Darvas + ATR)

This is like Donchian/Box breakout with ATR buffer.

Buy: When price breaks last 20-day high + (1 × ATR).

Sell: When price breaks last 20-day low – (1 × ATR).

Exit: Trail with 2 × ATR stop.

Best for: Positional trend riding in stocks like Reliance, Infosys, or commodities.

6. Multi-ATR Pyramid Strategy (for big moves)

Used by swing traders to ride trends and scale in safely.

Enter initial trade at breakout.

Add 2nd position if price moves in your favor by 1 × ATR.

Add 3rd position after another 1 × ATR.

Trail each position with 2 × ATR SL.

Best for: Riding big positional moves (gold, crude, indices).

7. ATR Mean-Reversion (Swing Fades)

Not always trend following—sometimes mean-reversion works.

If price moves more than 2.5 × ATR away from 20 SMA → expect pullback.

Buy when oversold candle appears below –2.5 × ATR.

Sell when overbought candle appears above +2.5 × ATR.

Best for: Swing fading over-extended stocks.

Practical Settings for Swing & Positional

ATR Periods: 14 (short), 21 (swing), 50 (positional).

Multipliers:

Intraday → 1–1.5× ATR

Swing → 1.5–2× ATR

Positional → 2–3× ATR

Key Takeaways

Use ATR not for direction but for risk and volatility adaptation.

Best working methods: ATR Breakouts, ATR Bands, ATR Stops, and ATR Position Sizing.

Swing traders → ATR breakout & bands.

Positional traders → ATR trend filter, ATR channel, and pyramiding.

Intraday Strategies Bible

1. Foundation of Intraday Trading

Goal: Capture moves within the same day (no overnight risk).

Key Pillars:

Market Bias → Trend, Range, or Reversal?

Volatility Measure → ATR or VWAP distance.

Timing → First 30 min, Mid-session, Closing hour.

Risk Control → Position sizing, SL, no overtrading.

2. Classic Intraday Strategies

📌 Strategy 1: Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

Concept: First 15/30 min sets the tone.

Steps:

Mark High & Low of first 15/30 min candle.

Go long if price breaks above High with volume.

Go short if price breaks below Low.

SL = Opposite side of range.

Target = 1.5× to 2× ATR of intraday.

Best on: Bank NIFTY, Crude Oil, high-beta stocks.

📌 Strategy 2: VWAP Reversion

Concept: VWAP = Fair value. Price oscillates around it intraday.

Steps:

If price extends 1.5–2× ATR away from VWAP → Mean reversion likely.

Enter long near VWAP support; short near VWAP resistance.

SL = 0.5× ATR.

Target = Return to VWAP.

Best during: Sideways or consolidating market.

📌 Strategy 3: ATR Breakout Scalping

Concept: Capture volatility bursts.

Steps:

Calculate 5-min ATR(14).

If a candle moves > 1.5× ATR with volume → Enter in direction of breakout.

SL = Half ATR below entry.

Exit at 1.5–2× ATR profit.

Works best: Around news, results, or macro events.

📌 Strategy 4: CPR Intraday Trend Strategy

Concept: CPR (Central Pivot Range) guides trend/bias.

Steps:

Narrow CPR → Expect trending day.

Wide CPR → Expect range day.

Long above CPR + bullish structure.

Short below CPR + bearish structure.

SL = Opposite CPR band.

Target = Next pivot level (R1/S1 or higher).

Best: NIFTY / Bank NIFTY.

📌 Strategy 5: Moving Average Crossovers (Intraday)

Concept: Short-term trend momentum.

Setup:

EMA 9 & EMA 21 on 5-min chart.

Long when EMA 9 crosses above EMA 21 + volume expansion.

Short when EMA 9 crosses below EMA 21.

SL = Recent swing low/high.

Exit at 1:2 RR or ATR-based trailing stop.

📌 Strategy 6: Pivot-to-Pivot Trading

Concept: Market moves between pivot levels intraday.

Steps:

Plot daily pivots (R1, R2, S1, S2).

Trade rejection or breakout of pivot with ATR filter.

SL = Half ATR beyond pivot.

Target = Next pivot level.

ATR Bands

1. What are ATR Bands?

ATR Bands = Price ± (ATR × Multiplier).

They expand/contract with volatility.

Unlike Bollinger Bands (which measure statistical deviation), ATR Bands directly reflect market range movement.

Formula:

Upper Band = Close + (ATR × Multiplier)

Lower Band = Close – (ATR × Multiplier)

2. Why Use ATR Bands?

Dynamic Support/Resistance → Helps you see where price may stretch intraday.

Breakout Filter → If price breaks above upper band with volume → trend continuation.

Mean Reversion → If price hits band extremes and stalls → pullback/reversal likely.

Volatility Tracking → Bands widen when market is active, shrink when consolidating.

3. Best ATR Band Settings

ATR Period

Default = 14 (Wilder).

For fast intraday (5-min, 15-min) → ATR(10–14).

For swing → ATR(20–21).

Multiplier

1.0 × ATR → Very tight bands, sensitive.

1.5 × ATR → Balanced (common).

2.0 × ATR → Wider, for strong trending markets.

👉 Intraday traders often use ATR(14) with 1.5–2 multiplier.

4. Trading Strategies with ATR Bands

📌 Strategy 1: Breakout Confirmation

If price closes above upper ATR band with volume → Long.

If price closes below lower ATR band → Short.

SL = Midpoint (Close) or opposite band.

Target = Trail with ATR bands.

📌 Strategy 2: Range Reversion

If price touches upper band but volume is weak → Short for mean reversion to midline.

If price touches lower band and stalls → Long back to mean.

📌 Strategy 3: Volatility Squeeze

Narrow ATR Bands = low volatility → Big move coming.

Watch for breakout direction → enter in breakout side.

📌 Strategy 4: Trend Riding

Use ATR Bands as dynamic trailing stop.

Long in uptrend → Trail SL at Lower ATR Band.

Short in downtrend → Trail SL at Upper ATR Band.

5. Pro Tips

Always combine ATR Bands with trend filter (EMA, VWAP, CPR).

Bands alone don’t tell you direction → they only show stretch potential.

Great for Bank NIFTY, NIFTY, Crude Oil, Gold (volatile instruments).

✅ Example (Bank NIFTY 5-min):

ATR(14) = 50 points.

Multiplier = 2.

Upper Band = Close + 100.

Lower Band = Close – 100.

If price breaks above upper band → strong bullish momentum.

ATR Trailing Stops Indicator

What is ATR Trailing Stops?

The ATR (Average True Range) Trailing Stop is a volatility-based stop loss system. Instead of using fixed points or percentages, it adapts to market volatility. When volatility is high, the stop widens to avoid false exits. When volatility is low, the stop tightens to protect profits.

It is widely used by swing traders, positional traders, and systematic trend followers.

How it Works

Calculate the ATR over a chosen period (commonly 14).

Multiply ATR by a multiplier (commonly 1.5, 2, or 3).

Subtract this value from price for long trades or add it for short trades.

Plot the stop level, which trails the price as it moves in your favor.

Formula:

For Long: Trailing Stop = Close – (ATR × Multiplier)

For Short: Trailing Stop = Close + (ATR × Multiplier)

Example

Stock Price: ₹500

ATR(14): ₹20

Multiplier: 2

For Long Trade: Trailing Stop = 500 – (20 × 2) = 460

If the stock moves to 550, with ATR still 20: Trailing Stop = 550 – 40 = 510

This stop keeps moving up but never comes down.

How to Use in Trading

1. Trend Following

Enter trade based on your strategy (moving average, breakout, etc.).

Use ATR trailing stop as exit rule.

Stay in the trend until price closes beyond the trailing stop.

2. Swing Trading

Use ATR trailing stop to lock in profits on 1–3 week trades.

Helps capture strong swing moves without exiting too early.

3. Intraday

Works well in trending intraday sessions.

For choppy sessions, reduce ATR period or multiplier.

Choosing Parameters

ATR Periods:

14 → standard, balanced

10 → faster, tighter stop

20–21 → slower, wider stop

Multipliers:

1.5 → aggressive, tighter stop (good for intraday)

2 → balanced (swing trades)

3 → conservative, wide stop (positional/trend following)

Advantages

Adapts to volatility, unlike fixed stops.

Prevents premature stop-outs in volatile markets.

Locks profits systematically.

Works across all assets (stocks, forex, commodities, indices).

Disadvantages

In sideways markets, can generate whipsaws.

Requires careful tuning of period and multiplier.

Not a standalone entry system (should be paired with a trend strategy).

Best Practices

Use ATR Trailing Stop as an exit system, not entry.

Combine with a trend filter (EMA, Supertrend, ADX).

Test different settings for each asset/timeframe.

Never reduce ATR multiplier below 1.5 for swing/positional, as it becomes too tight.
