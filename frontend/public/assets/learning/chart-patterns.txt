DELTA TO DHARMA

The Master Guide to Chart Patterns

1. What Are Chart Patterns?

Chart patterns are visual formations on price charts that signal how buyers and sellers are interacting. They reveal market psychology and help traders anticipate whether price will:

Reverse direction (Reversal patterns)

Continue the current trend (Continuation patterns)

Break either way (Bilateral patterns)

👉 Patterns are not predictions. They are probability signals that work best when combined with volume, trend context, and multi-timeframe analysis.

2. Classification of Chart Patterns

Reversal Patterns

1. Head and Shoulders (Top)

Definition: A bearish reversal pattern that forms at the end of an uptrend, signaling the shift from bullish to bearish momentum.

Formation:

Three peaks: Left Shoulder, Head (highest), Right Shoulder.

The “neckline” connects the troughs between shoulders.

Breakdown of the neckline confirms reversal.

Psychology:

Left Shoulder → Buyers strong, create new high.

Head → Buyers push further but struggle; demand weakens.

Right Shoulder → Buyers attempt again but fail to beat head.

Neckline Breakdown → Sellers take control.

Trading Approach:

Entry: On neckline breakdown (preferably with volume).

Stop Loss: Above right shoulder.

Target: Height from head to neckline projected downward.

Best Timeframes:

Daily/Weekly → Strong signals for positional/swing trades.

15m/1h → Works intraday but prone to false breakouts.

Tips & Hacks:

Volume typically falls at head and spikes on neckline breakdown.

Horizontal neckline = more reliable.

Steeper neckline → sharper move.

What If Scenarios:

Failed breakdown → price rallies above neckline = strong bullish continuation.

Right shoulder too small → weaker reliability.

2. Inverse Head and Shoulders (Bottom)

Definition: A bullish reversal pattern forming after a downtrend.

Formation:

Three troughs: middle one deeper (head), left & right shoulders shallow.

Breakout above neckline = reversal.

Psychology:

Sellers push strongly (head), but buying absorbs supply.

Right Shoulder → Sellers weaken.

Breakout → Buyers dominate.

Trading Approach:

Entry: On neckline breakout.

SL: Below right shoulder.

Target: Height from head to neckline projected upward.

Best Timeframes:

Daily/Weekly best for positional trades.

Lower TF (5m/15m) only works with confluence (VWAP/Volume).

Tips & Hacks:

Downward-sloping neckline gives stronger reversal.

RSI/MACD bullish divergence adds confirmation.

What If Scenarios:

Failed breakout → falls below neckline again → bearish continuation.

3. Double Top

Definition: A bearish reversal pattern where price fails twice at the same resistance.

Formation:

Two peaks near the same level.

Neckline = trough between them.

Breakdown confirms reversal.

Psychology:

First top → buyers push, sellers block.

Second top → buyers weaker, sellers defend.

Breakdown → sellers dominate.

Trading Approach:

Entry: On neckline breakdown.

SL: Above 2nd peak.

Target: Height from peaks to neckline.

Best Timeframes:

H1, H4, Daily.

Tips & Hacks:

Volume drops on 2nd top = weak buyers.

Peaks spaced apart = stronger pattern.

What If Scenarios:

If resistance breaks → bullish continuation instead.

4. Double Bottom

Definition: A bullish reversal pattern where support is tested twice and holds.

Formation:

Two troughs at similar levels.

Breakout above neckline = bullish.

Psychology:

First bottom → sellers strong, support holds.

Second bottom → sellers weaker, buyers step in.

Breakout → buyers take control.

Trading Approach:

Entry: On neckline breakout.

SL: Below 2nd bottom.

Target: Distance from bottoms to neckline.

Best Timeframes:

Daily & Weekly for swings.

15m/1h for intraday with volume confirmation.

Tips & Hacks:

RSI divergence strengthens setup.

Larger gap between bottoms = higher reliability.

What If Scenarios:

Failed breakout → sideways consolidation.

5. Triple Top / Triple Bottom

Definition: A stronger version of Double Top/Bottom.

Formation:

Price tests resistance/support three times.

Breakout confirms reversal.

Psychology:

Multiple failures exhaust trend.

Market prepares for reversal.

Trading Approach:

Same as double patterns but with bigger targets.

Best Timeframes:

H4, Daily, Weekly.

Tips & Hacks:

The longer it forms, the more powerful the breakout.

What If Scenarios:

If breakout happens opposite → invalidation.

6. Rising Wedge

Definition: A bearish reversal/continuation pattern where price rises within narrowing trendlines.

Formation:

Higher highs, higher lows converging.

Breakdown = bearish.

Psychology:

Buyers pushing but momentum fading.

Sellers wait for weakness to strike.

Trading Approach:

Entry: On breakdown.

SL: Above recent swing.

Target: Wedge height projected down.

Best Timeframes:

H1, H4, Daily.

Tips & Hacks:

Volume usually declines inside wedge.

Best used in overbought conditions.

What If Scenarios:

Upward breakout = fakeout → bullish continuation.

7. Falling Wedge

Definition: A bullish reversal/continuation where price falls in narrowing structure.

Formation:

Lower highs, lower lows converging.

Breakout upward = bullish.

Psychology:

Sellers weaken.

Buyers quietly accumulate.

Trading Approach:

Entry: On breakout.

SL: Below recent swing low.

Target: Height of wedge projected up.

Best Timeframes:

H1, H4, Daily.

Tips & Hacks:

Bullish divergence adds strength.

What If Scenarios:

Breakdown below support → bearish continuation.

8. Inverse Cup & Handle

Definition: A bearish reversal after a strong uptrend.

Formation:

Cup shape inverted (∩).

Handle = weak bullish pullback.

Breakdown = bearish continuation.

Psychology:

Buyers exhausted after long rally.

Handle = weak attempt to recover.

Breakdown → sellers take over.

Trading Approach:

Entry: Breakdown of handle support.

SL: Above handle high.

Target: Depth of cup projected downward.

Best Timeframes:

Daily/Weekly.

Tips & Hacks:

Heavy selling volume strengthens the move.

What If Scenarios:

If price breaks handle upward → bullish continuation.

Continuation Chart Patterns

1. Ascending Triangle

Definition: A bullish continuation pattern where price consolidates between a rising trendline (higher lows) and a flat resistance level.

Formation:

Resistance line: Horizontal, showing sellers at the same level.

Support line: Rising, showing buyers stepping in at higher levels.

Breakout typically happens upwards.

Psychology:

Sellers defend the resistance zone.

Buyers gradually push higher lows, showing strength.

Once resistance breaks → trapped sellers cover, buyers add → bullish rally.

Trading Approach:

Enter long on breakout with volume confirmation.

Stop-loss just below the rising support or last swing low.

Target = height of the triangle added to breakout point.

Best Timeframes:

Works best in higher timeframes (1H, 4H, Daily).

On smaller TFs, use it with volume confirmation.

Tips & Hacks:

The more times resistance is tested, the stronger the breakout.

Volume expansion = healthy breakout.

False breakouts are common → wait for candle close.

What If Scenarios:

If price breaks down instead of up → failed pattern → can signal reversal.

If price consolidates too long → breakout will be explosive.

2. Descending Triangle

Definition: A bearish continuation pattern where price consolidates between a flat support line and a falling resistance line.

Formation:

Support line: Horizontal, buyers holding at same level.

Resistance line: Falling, sellers stepping down aggressively.

Breakout usually downward.

Psychology:

Buyers defend the support but weaker with each test.

Sellers push lower highs, gaining control.

Once support breaks → panic selling.

Trading Approach:

Enter short on breakdown with volume confirmation.

Stop-loss above falling resistance.

Target = height of the triangle projected downwards.

Best Timeframes:

1H, 4H, Daily for reliability.

On intraday charts, combine with order flow or volume profile.

Tips & Hacks:

Works very well in bearish markets.

A breakdown with strong volume often leads to sharp moves.

What If Scenarios:

If price breaks up → failed pattern → can become bullish trap.

Extended sideways → support weakens more → bigger fall.

3. Symmetrical Triangle

Definition: A neutral continuation pattern with converging trendlines (lower highs + higher lows).

Formation:

Price squeezes into an apex.

Can break either way but usually follows trend direction.

Psychology:

Buyers and sellers balance out.

Market indecision → volatility squeeze.

Once breakout occurs → strong trend continuation.

Trading Approach:

Trade in direction of breakout with volume confirmation.

Stop-loss below last swing inside triangle.

Target = widest part of the triangle.

Best Timeframes:

Works across all TFs.

Very powerful on 4H/Daily.

Tips & Hacks:

If prior trend is strong, continuation probability is high.

Avoid trading inside the triangle → wait for breakout.

What If Scenarios:

If breakout is weak → false signal → wait for retest confirmation.

Long consolidation = stronger breakout.

4. Bullish Flag

Definition: Small downward-sloping consolidation in an uptrend.

Formation:

Strong bullish pole → small parallel downward channel → breakout upward.

Psychology:

Buyers take profit after rally.

Sellers step in briefly but weakly.

Buyers return → push higher.

Trading Approach:

Enter on breakout above flag.

Stop-loss below flag low.

Target = length of flagpole.

Best Timeframes:

15m, 1H for intraday.

4H, Daily for swing.

Tips & Hacks:

Best used after big impulsive moves.

Volume drops during flag → confirms pause.

What If Scenarios:

If flag breaks down → failed pattern → may indicate deeper pullback.

5. Bearish Flag

Definition: Small upward-sloping consolidation in a downtrend.

Formation:

Sharp bearish pole → parallel upward channel → breakout downward.

Psychology:

Sellers take a pause.

Buyers attempt rebound but weak.

Sellers resume dominance.

Trading Approach:

Enter short on breakdown.

Stop-loss above flag high.

Target = flagpole length.

Best Timeframes:

15m to 4H depending on trading style.

Tips & Hacks:

Ideal in strong downtrends.

Watch for breakdown volume.

What If Scenarios:

If it breaks upward → failed flag → trend exhaustion possible.

6. Bullish Pennant

Definition: Small symmetrical triangle after a sharp rally.

Formation:

Flagpole up → tiny triangle → breakout up.

Psychology:

Similar to flag but converging structure.

Buyers dominate after pause.

Trading Approach:

Enter long on breakout.

Stop-loss below pennant low.

Target = pole length.

Best Timeframes:

5m, 15m for scalping.

1H, 4H, Daily for swings.

Tips & Hacks:

Works best in strong momentum moves.

What If Scenarios:

Weak breakout → possible bull trap.

7. Bearish Pennant

Definition: Small symmetrical triangle after a sharp fall.

Formation:

Pole down → small triangle → breakout down.

Psychology:

Sellers dominate after pause.

Buyers try to hold but fail.

Trading Approach:

Enter short on breakdown.

Stop-loss above pennant high.

Target = pole length.

Best Timeframes:

5m–4H, especially strong in bearish momentum markets.

Tips & Hacks:

Combine with volume for confirmation.

What If Scenarios:

If breaks upward → reversal instead of continuation.

⚖️ Bilateral Patterns

Symmetrical Triangle (Bilateral – depends on context)

Definition A chart pattern where price consolidates with lower highs and higher lows, forming a triangle that converges towards an apex. It shows balance between buyers and sellers until a breakout occurs.

Formation

Both trendlines slope towards each other.

Volumes usually decline as the pattern progresses.

Breakout occurs near the final 1/3rd of the pattern.

Psychology

Market is in equilibrium: neither bulls nor bears dominate.

Every rally is weaker (lower highs) and every selloff is weaker (higher lows).

Pressure builds until one side breaks decisively.

Trading Approach

Wait for breakout (upside or downside) with strong volume.

Place stop-loss just outside opposite trendline.

Measure target = widest part of triangle projected from breakout.

Best Timeframes

Daily or Weekly for positional trades.

15-min or 1-hour for intraday setups.

Tips & Hacks

Trade in the direction of the prior trend (continuation bias).

Avoid guessing direction inside the triangle.

Volume spike on breakout = stronger reliability.

What If Scenarios

Breakout fails → whipsaw, retest of opposite trendline.

Breakout with low volume → false signal, wait for retest confirmation.

2. Wedges (Continuation or Reversal depending on context)

Definition Wedges are sloping patterns where both trendlines converge, but unlike triangles, the slope is either upward or downward.

Formation

Rising Wedge → Price moves up but with converging highs & lows.

Falling Wedge → Price moves down with converging highs & lows.

Psychology

Rising wedge: momentum fades in an uptrend, buyers are weakening.

Falling wedge: selling pressure reduces, sellers are weakening.

Acts as continuation in strong trends but can reverse in exhausted trends.

Trading Approach

Rising wedge → Bearish bias → short below support line.

Falling wedge → Bullish bias → long above resistance line.

Stop-loss just outside wedge boundary.

Target = height of wedge projected from breakout.

Best Timeframes

1-hour, 4-hour, or daily for reliability.

Avoid 1-min/5-min due to noise.

Tips & Hacks

Works best when aligned with trend + volume confirmation.

Rising wedge in downtrend = high-probability continuation.

Falling wedge in uptrend = high-probability continuation.

What If Scenarios

False breakout → If price re-enters wedge, avoid trade.

Too long wedge (stretched) → often fails, better skip.

3. Broadening Formation (Megaphone – Bilateral/Continuation)

Definition A rare pattern where the price action widens out like a megaphone, with higher highs and lower lows.

Formation

At least 3 touches on each side needed.

Expanding volatility over time.

Can occur in both uptrends and downtrends.

Psychology

Market is extremely volatile and indecisive.

Both buyers and sellers are aggressive, pushing price wider.

Often linked to high news flow or speculative environment.

Trading Approach

Aggressive traders: Buy at support, sell at resistance (range play).

Conservative traders: Wait for breakout beyond formation.

Targets = height of last swing projected outward.

Best Timeframes

4-hour and daily preferred.

Avoid low timeframes due to excessive noise.

Tips & Hacks

Avoid trading in the middle → always near boundary.

Volume usually increases with swings → confirms expansion.

After breakout, trend is often very strong.

What If Scenarios

Breakout without volume → Fake-out likely.

Pattern too large → treat as broad market indecision, better skip.

High volatility events (earnings, news) → pattern failure risk high.



🌟 Complex / Long-Term Patterns

Rounding Bottom (Saucer)

Long accumulation → slow bullish reversal.

Best on weekly/monthly charts.

Broadening Top/Bottom

Expanding highs/lows → extreme volatility.

Often seen near market tops.

3. Multi-Timeframe Usage

Chart patterns are fractal in nature — they appear on all timeframes, from 1-minute to monthly. However, their reliability and risk-reward vary with timeframe.

🔹 Intraday (1–15 min)

Behavior: Patterns form and resolve quickly. Good for scalpers but prone to fakeouts.

Best Use: Quick profit trades, scalping breakouts, fading false moves.

Caution: Higher noise, requires strict stop-losses and discipline.

🔹 Hourly (30m–1H)

Behavior: More stable than intraday, captures swing movements.

Best Use: Swing trades lasting 1–3 days. Balanced between speed and reliability.

Caution: Patterns may break late due to overnight gaps or news.

🔹 Daily / Weekly

Behavior: Strongest and most reliable. Represents institutional-level accumulation or distribution.

Best Use: Positional trades, long-term investment entries, portfolio strategy.

Caution: Larger stop-loss requirement, patience needed.

👉 Golden Rule:

Higher timeframe = stronger pattern reliability.

Use higher TF for direction bias and lower TF for precise entries. (E.g., if you see a Flag on Daily, drop to 15m to catch breakout with tight stop.)

4. Pattern Psychology (Deep Dive)

Behind every chart pattern is a battle of buyers and sellers. Recognizing who is trapped reveals the true opportunity.

Head & Shoulders: Buyers attempt higher highs (head) but fail. Each rally gets weaker. Sellers seize momentum → breakdown.

Double Bottom: Sellers push to support twice. Both attempts fail → buyers gain conviction → breakout higher.

Flags: Strong rally → pause (buyers resting, weak sellers enter). Once sellers exhaust, strong buyers push continuation.

Wedges: Trend loses momentum gradually. The losing side (trend followers) gets trapped → reversal or weak continuation.

Cup & Handle: Long correction (cup) → stabilization → shallow pullback (handle). Buyers reload → breakout.

👉 Key Question for Any Pattern: “Who is trapped — buyers or sellers?” Trapped traders provide the fuel for breakouts (they are forced to exit, adding momentum).

5. Practical Trading Rules

Patterns are only as strong as the discipline of execution. Follow these rules like a checklist:

Wait for Breakout Confirmation

Always wait for a candle close beyond support/resistance (not just a wick).

Volume Confirmation

High volume = strong breakout (institutional activity).

Weak volume = risk of fakeout.

Retest Entry = Safer Entry

Wait for price to pull back and retest breakout zone.

Gives better risk-reward and avoids chasing.

Stop-Loss Placement

Place SL outside the structure (below support for bullish, above resistance for bearish).

Never place SL inside the pattern.

Target Calculation

Height Projection: Measure pattern height, project from breakout point.

Fibonacci Extensions: Use 1.272, 1.618 levels.

Supply/Demand Zones: Next liquidity pool acts as natural target.

Trend Context Alignment

Continuation patterns work best in trending markets.

Avoid in sideways/choppy conditions (more failures).

6. Common Mistakes & Traps

❌ Entering before breakout confirms. ❌ Ignoring volume → falling for fakeouts. ❌ Forcing patterns in sideways chop. ❌ Trading patterns against larger trend. ❌ Placing SL too tight inside the pattern.

7. Combining Patterns with Indicators

Patterns work best with confirmations:

Volume: Breakouts with high volume = strong. Low volume = fakeout risk.

RSI / MACD Divergence: Confirms reversals (e.g., Double Bottom + RSI bullish divergence).

Moving Averages: Patterns above 200 EMA → stronger bullish setups.

Volume Profile:

HVN (high volume node): Price consolidates longer.

LVN (low volume node): Breakouts move faster.

Example: Ascending Triangle breakout near LVN can trigger explosive rallies.

✅ Final Checklist for Pattern Trading

✔ Identify type (Reversal / Continuation / Bilateral). ✔ Confirm breakout with volume + candle close. ✔ Align with higher timeframe trend. ✔ Use retest entries for safety. ✔ Calculate targets via classical + S/R zones. ✔ Watch for fakeouts & traps. ✔ Combine with RSI, MACD, MAs, Volume Profile. ✔ Manage risk — never risk more than 1–2% per trade.

🎯 Conclusion

Chart patterns are the visual DNA of markets — a map of buyer and seller psychology. When combined with multi-timeframe analysis, volume confirmation, and risk management, they become one of the most reliable ways to trade price action.

Used blindly, they fail. Used with context, they give traders a statistical edge that compounds over time.
