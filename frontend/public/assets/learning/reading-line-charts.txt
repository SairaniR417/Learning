INTRODUCTION TO LINE CHART

In financial markets, price moves constantly. It fluctuates, reacts, spikes, retraces, and consolidates. Yet, at the end of every time period—whether it is one minute, one day, or one month—the market makes a final decision. That decision is called the closing price.

A line chart exists to represent this final decision.

Unlike candlestick charts or bar charts, which display multiple data points (open, high, low, close), a line chart focuses on only one thing:

Where did the market finally agree to close?

This makes the line chart the cleanest, most honest, and least emotional way of viewing price.

Professional traders, portfolio managers, and institutional participants may execute trades using advanced tools, but when they analyze trend, structure, and long-term bias, they mentally think in line charts.

Downtrend

Range
