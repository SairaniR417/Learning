💡 Core Idea

They tell us why an option gains or loses value even when the market seems quiet.

⚙️ 1️⃣ Delta (Δ) — The Directional Compass

🔍 Concept:

Delta measures how much an option’s price changes for a ₹1 change in the underlying.

[ \Delta = \frac{\text{Change in Option Price}}{\text{Change in Underlying Price}} ]

Call Options → Delta between 0 and +1

Put Options → Delta between 0 and –1

🧠 Intuition:

Delta shows:

Sensitivity – how fast your option reacts to movement

Probability – chance of finishing in-the-money at expiry

💬 Trader Insights:

High Delta → Predictability (behaves like futures)

Low Delta → Leverage (cheap but low odds)

ATM Delta (≈ 0.5) = equilibrium point — where acceleration begins.

⚙️ 2️⃣ Theta (Θ) — The Clock That Eats Premium

🔍 Concept:

Theta measures how much an option loses daily just because time passes — assuming price and volatility remain unchanged.

[ \Theta = \frac{\text{Change in Premium}}{\text{Change in Time}} ]

Always negative for buyers (they lose time value)

Always positive for sellers (they gain from decay)

💬 Trader Insights:

Buyers must be right in direction and in time.

Sellers can be right just by waiting.

Theta = patience premium.

⚙️ 3️⃣ Gamma (Γ) — The Accelerator of Delta

🔍 Concept:

Gamma measures the speed at which Delta changes when the underlying moves.

[ \Gamma = \frac{\text{Change in Delta}}{\text{Change in Underlying Price}} ]

If Delta is your car’s speed, Gamma is its acceleration.

📈 Example:

ATM 22,000 CE has:

Δ = 0.50

Γ = 0.02

If NIFTY rises ₹50 → New Δ = 0.50 + (0.02 × 50) = 0.60 → Option becomes more sensitive to next moves.

💬 Behavior:

High Gamma = faster reaction, more volatility in option P/L.

ATM options have maximum Gamma, especially near expiry.

Sellers fear Gamma; buyers love it.

⚙️ 4️⃣ Vega (ν) — The Volatility Valve

🔍 Concept:

Vega measures how much the option’s price changes for a 1% change in Implied Volatility (IV).

[ \nu = \frac{\text{Change in Premium}}{\text{Change in IV (%)}} ]

High Vega → more sensitive to volatility shocks.

Vega is highest for ATM options, lowest for deep ITM/OTM.

🧩 Example:

NIFTY 22,000 CE @ ₹180, Vega = 8 → IV rises from 15% → 16% → Premium ≈ ₹188 (+₹8)

Even without price movement, the premium increased — that’s Vega power.

🧠 Volatility Interpretation:

IV (Implied Volatility) = Market’s forecast of future movement.

High IV = Fear, uncertainty, or event risk (like Budget, Fed, results).

Low IV = Calm markets, low option prices.

💬 Trader Insights:

Option Buyers want IV to rise after entry (they gain from inflated Vega).

Option Sellers want IV to collapse (they profit from Vega crush).

🧠 Rule:

“Buy when IV is low and expected to rise. Sell when IV is high and expected to fall.”

📉 Volatility in Context (Intro to VIX):

The India VIX is a volatility index derived from NIFTY options.

Rising VIX = rising fear → costlier options.

Falling VIX = calm → cheaper options.

⚙️ 5️⃣ Rho (ρ) — The Interest Rate Sensitivity

🔍 Concept:

Rho measures how much an option’s price changes for a 1% change in interest rates.

Call → Positive Rho (benefits from rising rates)

Put → Negative Rho (benefits from falling rates)

🧠 Impact is minimal for weekly/monthly options, but relevant for long-term LEAPS.
