Bollinger Bands

1. Origin & Background

Invented by John Bollinger in the early 1980s.

Purpose: To create a dynamic indicator that adapts to volatility instead of using fixed overbought/oversold levels.

Based on the principle: Markets move in cycles of volatility → contraction (quiet) → expansion (violent).

2. Structure & Formula

Bollinger Bands consist of three lines:

Middle Band (MB) → 20-period Simple Moving Average (default).

Upper Band (UB) → MB + (2 × Standard Deviation).

Lower Band (LB) → MB – (2 × Standard Deviation).

Formula:

MB = SMA (Close, 20)

UB = MB + k × StdDev (20)

LB = MB – k × StdDev (20) (Default: k = 2).

3. Purpose

To identify relative high/low prices.

To measure volatility expansions and contractions.

To help traders with trend following and mean reversion.

4. Interpretation

Squeeze → Bands narrow → volatility low → big move coming.

Expansion → Bands widen → volatility high → trend in action.

Band Touch →

Upper Band touch ≠ sell immediately. It shows strength.

Lower Band touch ≠ buy blindly. It shows weakness.

Context matters.

Walk the Bands → In strong trends, price hugs bands for a long time.

5. Strategies

(A) Bollinger Band Squeeze Breakout

Identify narrow bands.

Entry: Breakout candle close outside band.

SL: Opposite band.

Target: 2x risk or ride trend with trailing stop.

(B) Mean Reversion Trade

Price pierces upper/lower band.

RSI/Stochastic divergence appears.

Entry: Opposite to breakout.

Target: 20 SMA (mean reversion).

(C) Trend Rider Strategy

Identify strong trend (price above 20 SMA).

Buy pullbacks near middle band.

SL = below lower band.

Trail stop with band expansion.

6. Pro-Level Secrets

Double Bottom inside Band = bullish reversal.

Double Top inside Band = bearish reversal.

Band Width Indicator → Use to time squeezes mathematically.

Multi-Timeframe Power → Daily squeeze + 15-min trigger = explosive move.

Volume Confirmation → Breakout with high volume = genuine.

7. Best Use Cases

Swing trading (2–10 days).

Intraday breakout trading.

Identifying volatility cycles before earnings/news events.

8. Mistakes to Avoid

Selling every touch of upper band (trending market trap).

Ignoring broader market structure (S/R, supply-demand zones).

Forgetting volatility cycle principle (quiet → violent).

Institutional Insights

1. Volatility Clustering & Mean Reversion

Institutions don’t just look at BB visually; they use volatility models (ARCH/GARCH, realized vol, implied vol).

Bollinger Bands are a simplified retail-friendly proxy of that.

Smart money uses BB squeezes to identify volatility clustering → when realized volatility is at extreme lows, they anticipate large moves and position ahead (like straddles in options).

2. Options Market Connection

Market Makers in options use bands to estimate IV crush / IV expansion.

If price is at the upper band AND IV is stretched, they hedge short gamma positions by buying/selling futures.

That’s why breakouts after squeezes often align with option writers unwinding hedges.

3. Liquidity Pools & Stop Hunts

When price hugs the band, retail traders fade it (sell upper, buy lower).

Institutions use this retail bias → they push price slightly beyond the band to trigger stops (band breakout) and then take the opposite side.

Example: "Band fakeouts" are liquidity hunts by algos.

4. Institutional Tweaks

They don’t use fixed 20 SMA. Instead:

Adaptive moving averages (Kaufman AMA, VWAP).

Volatility multipliers tied to ATR, HV, or implied vol instead of simple StdDev.

Multi-timeframe bandwidth analysis to track where volatility is about to expand (daily squeeze → intraday breakout).

5. Real Application

Funds use BB for risk management overlays:

Example: If price closes > 2.5σ from mean → cut exposure, hedge, or rebalance.

Some quants use BB to normalize price → converting price action into Z-scores for statistical arbitrage.
