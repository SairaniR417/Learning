📗 MODULE 4: FAIR VALUE GAPS (FVGs) & ORDER BLOCKS (OBs)

The Precision Zones Where Institutions Enter and Exit

🧩 4.1 What is a Fair Value Gap (FVG)?

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is a three-candle pattern that shows imbalance between buyers and sellers.

It’s formed when price moves too quickly, leaving a gap between:

Candle 1’s high and

Candle 3’s low (for bullish FVG) or vice versa for bearish FVG.

📊 Visual: Candle 1–2–3 FVG illustration.

📘 Example:

If price rallies fast with three big green candles, and the middle candle’s body doesn’t overlap with the first and third — that space is an FVG.

That means price moved so fast that no trades happened in between. Later, price returns to fill part or full of that gap.

⚙️ 4.2 Why FVGs Form

Because of Displacement — when one side (buyers/sellers) overpowers the other, leaving behind inefficiency.

Institutions need to rebalance those zones later by revisiting them — that’s why FVGs act like magnets.

🧠 4.3 How to Identify FVGs

Look for three candles: (1) push candle, (2) impulse candle, (3) pullback candle.

Measure gap between candle 1’s high and candle 3’s low.

If there’s space → that’s your FVG zone.

Bullish FVG: between 1’s high and 3’s low. Bearish FVG: between 1’s low and 3’s high.

🧭 4.4 Market Reaction to FVGs

After a displacement:

Price often retraces into the FVG.

Fills the inefficiency (partially or fully).

Continues in the original direction.

This retracement is where Smart Money re-enters the trend.

🔹 4.5 Valid vs Invalid FVGs

✅ Valid FVG:

Created by strong displacement.

Aligns with BOS or CHOCH.

Has clear liquidity grab before formation.

❌ Invalid FVG:

Small candles with overlap.

No liquidity taken before formation.

Occurs in choppy zones.

⚡ 4.6 FVG & Market Structure

FVGs form the core zones where structure continuation happens.

Example:

Price breaks structure (BOS).

Creates displacement (strong move).

Forms FVG → retracement into it → continuation.

This is the Smart Money Continuation Model.

🧱 4.7 What are Order Blocks (OBs)?

An Order Block is the last opposite candle before a major displacement move.

It represents the exact price zone where Smart Money executed large orders to reverse or continue price.

📊 Visual: Bullish and Bearish OB Example.

🔸 Types of Order Blocks:

Type

Definition

Example

Bullish OB

Last bearish candle before upward displacement

Base before big rally

Bearish OB

Last bullish candle before downward displacement

Base before crash

📉 4.8 Why OBs Work

Smart Money uses these candles to place bulk institutional orders. After a displacement, price revisits these OBs to:

Mitigate remaining orders.

Test reaction (buy/sell pressure).

Continue in the same direction.

That’s why OBs often align with FVGs and Fibonacci levels (0.5–0.618 retracements).

🔍 4.9 Rules for Valid Order Blocks

✅ Large displacement follows after OB. ✅ Candle closes strongly beyond structure. ✅ Liquidity is swept before OB forms. ✅ No overlapping structure inside OB. ✅ Price later respects OB during pullback.

If price breaks through the OB → it’s invalidated.

💡 4.10 FVG + OB + Liquidity Confluence

The most powerful setups combine:

Liquidity grab (equal highs/lows taken)

Displacement (clear intent)

FVG zone (imbalance target)

Order Block (institutional re-entry)

Fibonacci 0.5–0.618 (confirmation zone)

That’s the DeltaToDharma Institutional Entry Model.

⚙️ 4.11 How to Trade FVGs and OBs

🔹 Bullish Scenario:

Identify liquidity sweep below lows.

Look for displacement up → FVG + Bullish OB.

Wait for pullback into FVG or OB near 0.5–0.618 zone.

Enter long with confirmation candle.

Stop below OB, target next liquidity high.

🔹 Bearish Scenario:

Liquidity grab above highs.

Displacement down → FVG + Bearish OB.

Pullback to OB or 61.8 zone.

Enter short with confirmation.

Stop above OB, target next low.

🧘‍♂️ 4.12 The Institutional Mindset

Smart Money never chases price — they let price return to their footprints (OB & FVG).

As a trader, your role is not to predict — but to wait for price to return to value zones.

“Retail traders chase candles. Smart Money waits at their own orders.”
