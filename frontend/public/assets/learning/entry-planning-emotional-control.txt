1. Core Philosophy – “Sacred” Means Ritual

In D2D, your entry process is like a pilgrim’s prayer before a journey — precise, unshaken, and repeated exactly the same way each time.

Why Rituals Matter in Trading:

Removes emotional shortcuts.

Reduces decision fatigue.

Increases consistency → consistency builds trust → trust builds performance.

EXAMPLE (Sacred Checklist for a Pullback Trader): ✅ Trend confirmed on higher timeframe ✅ Price at zone + respected last 3 times ✅ Candle pattern forms in my favor ✅ Volume rising on the bounce ✅ RR ≥ 1:2

2. Understanding Entry Triggers

Think of triggers as market invitations — not all invites are worth attending.

A. Breakouts – The Door Bursts Open

What: Price breaks a clear resistance (long) or support (short).

When It Works Best: In trending markets with strong participation.

Trick: Wait for a close above the level + above-average volume. Enter on retest if you’re conservative.

Hack: Look for “coiling” price action before breakout — smaller candles mean pressure is building.

Example: NIFTY 50 is stuck between 20000–20050 for 3 sessions. At 10:20 AM, it closes above 20050 with 2× average 5-min volume. VWAP bounce confirms trend strength → Enter long.

B. Pullbacks – The Market Comes to You

What: Price retraces to a known support in an uptrend or resistance in a downtrend.

When It Works Best: In clean, steady trends without erratic spikes.

Trick: Use Fibonacci retracements (38.2%–50%) for high-probability zones.

Hack: Add RSI 50-level cross as confirmation for momentum resumption.

Example: Reliance rallies from ₹2500 to ₹2600, then pulls back to ₹2570 (20 EMA + 38.2% Fib). Bullish engulfing + rising volume on bounce = entry.

C. Reversals – The Tide Turns

What: Price changes direction after exhausting a move.

When It Works Best: At higher timeframe zones or after sharp trends.

Trick: Look for divergence between price and indicators (RSI, MACD).

Hack: Combine “2X Confirmation” — reversal candle pattern + break of minor structure.

Example: Bank NIFTY falls for 4 straight days into a weekly demand zone. Hammer candle forms, RSI diverges (price lower, RSI higher), next candle breaks hammer high → enter long.

3. Confluence Stacking – The D2D Triangle

The market rewards alignment of forces. In D2D, we want at least 2 out of 3 confluences in our favor before pulling the trigger.

The D2D Triangle:

Price Action – Levels, zones, candle patterns, structure breaks.

Indicator Confirmation – EMA, VWAP, MACD, RSI, ADX.

Volume Pulse – Above-average activity shows real commitment.

Trick: Your weakest trades will come when you rely on only one of these three. Log and review — you’ll see the pattern.

Hack: If you find all 3 in alignment on both your execution and higher timeframe — double the conviction (and maybe position size).

Example (3/3 Confluence Long):

Price breaks daily resistance.

20 EMA above 50 EMA + MACD bullish crossover.

Volume 1.8× average. → This is a sacred setup.

4. The False Signal Filter

Markets are experts at baiting you. Your filter is your spiritual firewall.

Filters:

❌ No entry in the middle of a range.

❌ No entry counter to higher timeframe trend without 2 strong reversal confirmations.

❌ No entry with flat or falling volume.

❌ No entry during chop hours (often first 5 mins after open or lunch lull).

❌ Avoid just before big news unless that’s your playbook.

Tricks:

HTF Scan Rule: Always look one timeframe higher before entering.

Market Mood Check: Use an index chart to see if overall sentiment supports your trade.

Hack: Create a pre-trade traffic light system: 🟢 All conditions met → Take it 🟡 1–2 doubts → Reduce size 🔴 Multiple doubts → Skip

5. Entry Risk Sizing Logic (The Sacred Math)

The difference between pros and gamblers? Pros size to risk.

Formula:

Position Size = (Account Risk %) × (Account Balance) ÷ (Stop-Loss in Points × Point Value)

Tricks:

Fix your risk per trade (1–2%).

Reduce size, not stop-loss distance, for volatile setups.

For options, size based on premium move, not underlying.

Example: ₹5,00,000 capital, risk = 1%, stop-loss = 20 points, point value = ₹50.

Size = (0.01 × 5,00,000) ÷ (20 × 50)

Size = ₹5,000 ÷ ₹1,000 = 5 lots

Hack: Pre-calculate lot sizes for common stop-loss distances and keep them in a ready-reference table.
