Volume Profile

1. What is Volume Profile?

Definition: Volume Profile is a charting tool that plots traded volume at each price level, instead of just showing volume by time (the standard volume bars at bottom of chart).

It gives a horizontal histogram across price levels, showing where market participants are most active.

Unlike traditional volume, it tells us “at what prices” volume occurred, not just “how much volume per candle.”

👉 Think of it as the footprint of buyers and sellers across price.

2. Key Components of Volume Profile

a) Point of Control (POC)

The price level with the highest traded volume in that session/period.

Acts like a magnet for price (mean-reversion) but also a strong support/resistance zone.

b) Value Area (VA)

The region where 70% of the total volume has occurred.

Two levels form:

Value Area High (VAH)

Value Area Low (VAL)

Traders often use VAH as resistance and VAL as support.

c) Nodes (Volume Nodes)

High Volume Node (HVN):

Areas where a lot of trading happened.

Market has accepted this price = "fair value zone."

Price tends to consolidate here.

Low Volume Node (LVN):

Areas with little trading activity.

Market rejected these prices quickly.

Price tends to move through LVNs fast, often used for breakout/imbalance trades.

3. Characteristics of HVN & LVN

HVN (Acceptance Zones):

Act like “magnets” pulling price.

Price slows down, consolidates, or chops here.

Good for range trades.

LVN (Rejection Zones):

Act like barriers or “walls.”

Price often bounces or slices quickly through them.

Good for breakout trades.

4. Market Profile vs Volume Profile (Quick Clarification)

Market Profile: Based on time at price (letters/TPO).

Volume Profile: Based on actual traded volume.

Volume Profile is considered more accurate for modern trading (since institutions move volume, not just time).

5. Shapes & Structures of Volume Profile

Each day/session leaves behind a shape that tells a story of auction (buyer-seller battle).

a) D-Shape (Balanced Profile)

Volume centered around POC.

Market is in balance/fair value.

Next day expectation: Range-bound until breakout.

b) P-Shape Profile (Short Covering / Bullish Bias)

Looks like letter “P” → more volume at higher prices.

Indicates shorts trapped and covering → bullish bias.

Next day expectation: Continuation upward or at least stay above VAH.

c) b-Shape Profile (Long Liquidation / Bearish Bias)

Looks like letter “b” → more volume at lower prices.

Indicates longs trapped and exiting → bearish bias.

Next day expectation: Continuation downward or at least stay below VAL.

d) Double Distribution Profile

Two distinct peaks (two HVNs).

Market couldn’t agree → price accepted at two different zones.

Next day expectation: Price may oscillate between the two HVNs.

6. How to Use Volume Profile in Trading

a) Intraday Trading

POC re-tests: Price often revisits POC during the day.

VAH/VAL rejections: Watch for rejection at Value Area edges.

LVN breakouts: Price moves quickly through LVN zones.

b) Swing Trading

Identify bigger HVNs (weekly/monthly) → these are institutional accumulation zones.

Buy near VAL in uptrends, sell near VAH in downtrends.

c) Long-Term Investing

Monthly/quarterly profiles show institutional footprints.

HVNs on higher timeframes often act as long-term support/resistance.

7. How It Helps Predict Next Move

If price accepts above VAH → expect bullish continuation.

If price rejects VAH → mean reversion to POC.

If profile forms P-shape → bullish short covering.

If profile forms b-shape → bearish long liquidation.

If double distribution → watch the LVN between two HVNs as key decision zone.

8. Institutional Insights (Why This Works)

Institutions accumulate/distribute large orders at HVNs.

LVNs represent imbalances → big moves happened there.

Volume Profile shows you where institutions are active vs where market rejected price.

Trade like institutions — by focusing on where volume is, not just where price is.

D-Shape Profile

1. What is a D-shape?

Structure: Symmetrical, like a “bell curve.”

POC sits in the middle, with roughly equal distribution above and below.

Psychology: Market is in balance / fair value. Buyers and sellers agree → price is accepted.

Bias: Neutral until breakout.

👉 Institutions accumulate and distribute here → it’s often a pause before a trend.

2. Key Characteristics

Balanced distribution = no side dominates.

POC is central = magnet for price the next day.

Price often oscillates between VAH (Value Area High) and VAL (Value Area Low).

Breakouts from D-shape → powerful moves (market moving from balance to imbalance).

3. Levels to Mark

POC (central magnet).

VAH / VAL (edges of balance).

Watch for LVNs just outside VAH/VAL → potential breakout zones.

4. Next-Day Expectation

Base case: Price rotates around POC, staying range-bound between VAH and VAL.

Key opportunity: When price breaks out of VAH or VAL → trend day possible.

5. Opening Scenarios & Trade Playbook

A — Open inside Value Area

Bias: Neutral. Expect oscillation.

Plan:

Long near VAL → target POC/VAH.

Short near VAH → target POC/VAL.

Avoid trading in the middle (chop zone).

Stops: Just beyond VAH/VAL with buffer.

B — Open above VAH (gap up)

Bias: bullish if price holds above VAH.

Plan:

If acceptance above VAH → buy continuation.

If rejection and fall back into VA → fade short toward POC.

C — Open below VAL (gap down)

Bias: bearish if price holds below VAL.

Plan:

If acceptance below VAL → short continuation.

If reclaim back inside VA → fade long toward POC.

6. Entry, Stop, Target Logic

Entry:

Fades at VAH/VAL edges (when inside balance).

Breakout retest at VAH/VAL (when imbalance begins).

Stop:

Just outside VAH/VAL.

For breakouts: just inside the balance area.

Targets:

Inside VA = opposite edge (VAL or VAH).

Breakouts = next LVN or HVN outside.

7. Confirmation

Inside VA: Low volume, oscillating trade → fading edges works best.

Breakout: Need strong volume outside VAH/VAL → confirms trend move.

Invalidation: Breakout fails → expect sharp reversal back into balance.

8. Multi-Day / Swing View

Consecutive D-shapes → prolonged accumulation/distribution.

Once broken, bigger directional move expected (balance → imbalance transition).

Weekly/monthly D-shapes = major institutional positioning zones.

9. Example Trade (NIFTY-style)

Suppose today formed D-shape: POC = 25,000, VAH = 25,100, VAL = 24,900.

Tomorrow, open = 25,020 (inside VA).

Plan: If price rises to VAH (25,100) and rejects → short at 25,095.

Stop = 25,120.

Target 1 = POC (25,000).

Target 2 = VAL (24,900).

Risk/Reward ≈ 1:4.

10. Quick Checklist for D-shape Day After

✔ Mark POC, VAH, VAL. ✔ Expect sideways unless breakout. ✔ Inside VA → fade edges (VAH short, VAL long). ✔ Above VAH → continuation if accepted, fade if rejected. ✔ Below VAL → continuation if accepted, fade if rejected. ✔ Use volume to confirm breakout vs rejection.

🔑 Wrap

D-shape = balance day → next day usually range trading around POC.

Best trades = fade edges inside balance or catch breakout outside VAH/VAL.

Invalidation = strong volume acceptance beyond VAH/VAL → trend begins.

B-shape

1) Quick recap — what a B-shape tells you

B-shape = heavy volume concentrated at lower prices (the “belly” is low).

Classic interpretation: long liquidation / selling pressure → short-term bearish bias.

Price accepted lower levels during that session (HVN at lower prices) and likely leaves less volume above (LVNs above) — so upward moves can be rejected, downward moves can continue faster.

Important: B-shape gives probability not certainty. Use confirmation (price action + volume) before putting on large trades.

2) First things to draw before market open (the essentials)

Previous day POC (highest volume price) — primary magnet.

Previous day VAH / VAL (Value Area High / Low — 70% VA).

Main HVN(s) and LVN(s) — mark HVN zones (wider) and LVN gaps (thin).

Daily high / low and any visible overnight gaps.

Higher-TF HVNs (weekly/monthly) if they’re close — they can override intraday signals.

Add VWAP and a couple of short EMAs (e.g., 20 EMA on 5/15m) for confluence.

3) Expectation map for tomorrow

Base case (most likely): bearish continuation or at least retest & rejection of higher prices.

How it usually plays out:

Price may pop up early (retest upper LVN/HVN or POC) then sell off once supply absorbs that retest.

Or price may gap down and move quickly through LVNs toward next HVN / support.

Watch volume: strong volume on downward moves = validated continuation.

4) Concrete trade plans — 3 opening scenarios & what to do

A — Opens above previous VAH (gap up / strong open)

Bias: neutral → small bullish edge intraday, but B-shape says the underlying session was distribution — be cautious.

Aggressive: fade the open if price rallies toward previous POC/HVN and shows rejection (pin bar, bearish engulfing) — enter short on rejection.

Entry: limit on rejection candle close (or market if momentum breaks).

Stop: just above the recent rejection wick or slightly above HVN (POC) + buffer.

Target 1: previous day POC or VAL. Target 2: next structural support / weekly HVN.

Conservative: wait for price to fall back into the Value Area. If price fails to hold above VAH and falls below POC, look to short on the break of POC/VAL.

B — Opens inside previous Value Area (most common)

Bias: bearish (because B-shape), but action depends on how price behaves around POC.

Plan:

If price retests POC/HVN and shows bearish PA → short with stop above POC.

If price slides below VAL with pickup in selling volume → short continuation (enter on break or retest as resistance).

If price instead accepts above POC (sustained above VAH on strong volume), discard bearish bias and wait for clear flip — treat as failed B-shape.

C — Opens below previous VAL (gap down)

Bias: strongly bearish — high probability for continuation.

Options:

Trend follow: enter on pullback to the broken VAL/LVN (now resistance) or on continuation breakdowns to new LVN.

Momentum entry: if the gap down is accompanied by heavy selling volume and price accelerates away from the gap, enter with a tight stop (aggressive).

Targets: first target = nearest HVN / high-volume pocket below (POC of the earlier distribution). If that breaks, look to weekly support/HVN.

5) Entry types, stop placement & target logic (practical rules)

Entry signals (use one or more for confluence):

Bearish price action at POC/HVN (rejection candle, bearish engulfing).

Breakdown below VAL or LVN with above-average volume.

Pullback to broken support (VAH/VAL/HVN) turning into resistance.

Stop placement:

Conservative: beyond the HVN/POC or the high of the rejection candle + a volatility buffer.

Tighter/aggressive: beyond recent swing high or wick (smaller size, for scalp).

Never place stop right at a node — give a few ticks/points buffer for spread/noise.

Targets:

Target 1 = previous session POC or nearest HVN below.

Target 2 = next structural support (daily low / weekly HVN).

Consider scaling out: take 50% at T1, trail stop to breakeven, let remainder run toward T2.

6) Volume confirmation & how to read it

Good confirmation for continuation: price moves through a LVN or VAL with increasing volume (shows aggressive selling).

Warning sign / false breakout: price breaks level on low volume or breaks and immediately returns above (a wick back into area) → likely a false move.

POC behavior:

POC holding as resistance after retest suggests continuation lower.

POC being reclaimed on strong buying volume invalidates short bias.

7) Timeframes & tools (what to watch live)

Intraday: 1m/5m/15m for entries; use session (daily) Volume Profile.

Swing: 1h/4h and daily profiles — use weekly profile to see where institutions sit.

Indicators to combine: VWAP (short term fair price), 20 EMA for trend, ATR for volatility (position sizing), and support/resistance.

Profiles: use daily session profile for immediate zones; overlay visible range for multiple session clusters; check weekly for major HVNs.

8) Example trade (numbers + position sizing calculation)

Example scenario (generic stock/asset — adapt to your instrument):

Account size = ₹200,000.

Risk per trade = 1% → risk amount = 200000 × 0.01 = 2000.

(Digit by digit: 2 0 0 0 0 0 × 0.01 = 2 0 0 0) = ₹2,000.

Say you plan a short with entry ₹1,000 and stop loss at ₹1,010 → risk per share = ₹10.

Position size = risk amount / risk per share = 2000 / 10 = 200 shares.

(Digit by digit: 2000 ÷ 10 = 200)

If targeting 20 points (₹20) → gross reward = 200 × 20 = ₹4,000 (R:R = 2:1).

Adjust numbers for futures/options (use tick value and lot size) or for indices — compute the same way but convert tick/lot to monetary risk.

9) Swing / multi-day interpretation (how B-shape affects longer term)

If B-shape repeats across multiple sessions → institutional distribution → higher probability of a larger downtrend.

Single B-shape in an otherwise bullish higher TF market = short-term opportunity only — treat as a scalp/trade, not a position reversal.

Use weekly/monthly HVNs as filters: if weekly HVN is far below and B-shape forms, larger TF support may slow/stop the fall.

10) Common traps & rules to avoid mistakes

Don’t short only because of the B-shape — require confirmation (POC rejection, breakdown below VAL/LVN, or volume spike).

Avoid large size on the open without confirmation — the open is noisy.

Respect higher TF context: a daily B in a clear multi-week uptrend has lower success for big short trades.

Mind news / events — session-defining prints around economic data can invalidate patterns.

11) Quick morning checklist (copy/paste for tomorrow)

Mark yesterday’s POC, VAH, VAL, HVNs and LVNs.

Note where overnight/weekly HVNs lie.

Note the open relative to VA (above/inside/below).

Watch first 30–60 minutes: is POC retested? rejection or acceptance?

If retest + rejection at POC/HVN → consider short with stop above HVN.

If break below VAL/LVN on high volume → trend short (enter on break or retest).

Size per risk rules and place stops with buffer beyond nodes/wicks.

Take partial profits at POC/HVN below; trail remainder.

12) Post-trade journal prompts (what to record)

Setup type (open scenario A/B/C), entry price, stop, target, size.

Which node or POC acted as support/resistance?

Volume behavior during trade (expanding/contracting).

Outcome and lesson: was bias right? Was move validated by volume? Did you respect higher TF?

Short wrap & final notes

B-shape = bearish edge tomorrow, but trade with structure: mark POC/VAH/VAL/HVNs/LVNs, wait for price action + volume confirmation, size properly, and use stops beyond nodes.

P-Shape Profile

1. What does a P-shape mean?

P-shape profile has a wide upper distribution (like the belly of P) and a thin lower tail.

It tells you:

Shorts (sellers) were trapped and forced to cover → creating strong upward movement.

Buyers then accepted higher prices (volume built at the top).

Market psychology: Long liquidation is over, sellers gave up, buyers are in control.

Bias: Bullish / Short-covering continuation.

2. Key Characteristics of P-shape

Thin lower tail = rejected lower prices quickly.

Wide high-volume zone near top = acceptance at higher levels.

POC sits toward the upper part of the profile, unlike B-shape where it sits low.

👉 Interpretation: Market is finding fair value higher. Next day has upward continuation bias unless proven otherwise.

3. Levels to Mark (before next day)

Previous day POC (important, strong support).

VAH / VAL (Value Area High & Low).

The single prints / LVN in the lower tail (important rejection zones).

Daily High/Low.

Weekly HVN/POC if nearby.

4. Next-Day Expectation Map

Bias: bullish.

Common behavior next day:

Price dips early toward POC/VAL → then bounces strongly (longs defend, shorts trapped again).

Price can gap up and continue trending higher.

If price opens inside VA but holds above POC → expect upside extension.

If price fails and breaks below VAL → bullish thesis weakens (caution: trend reversal).

5. Opening Scenarios & Trading Plans

A — Opens above VAH (gap up)

Bias: continuation of bullish move.

Aggressive plan: Buy pullback into VAH/POC zone.

Conservative plan: Wait for acceptance above VAH (consolidation or retest holding) → then buy breakout continuation.

Stops: Below VAH or below POC.

Targets: First resistance (swing high / weekly HVN). Scale out on strength.

B — Opens inside previous Value Area

Bias: bullish, but expect dip into POC/VAL.

Plan: Buy dip into POC/VAL zone if defended with strong rejection candles/volume pickup.

Invalidation: If price sustains below VAL → bullish scenario fails → step aside or prepare for short reversal.

C — Opens below VAL (gap down)

Bias: tricky. If quick reclaim of VAL → strong bullish continuation (short trap extended).

Plan:

If quick reclaim of VAL → aggressive buy, stop below gap low.

If sustain below VAL → invalidation → market has shifted bearish → avoid longs.

6. Entry & Exit Playbook

Entry signals:

Rejection candle / pin bar at POC or VAL.

Breakout retest of VAH.

Acceptance above VAH on high volume.

Stop placement:

Conservative: below VAL (for POC/VAH entries).

Aggressive: below rejection wick.

Add a small buffer for volatility.

Targets:

T1 = previous day high or VAH retest.

T2 = next HVN / resistance (daily/weekly).

Scale partial profits at T1, trail stop for remainder.

7. Volume Confirmation

Good bullish confirmation: Volume rises on up moves, falls on pullbacks.

Warning sign: Price above VAH but volume low → breakout may fail.

POC defense: Strong bounce from POC = buyers in control.

8. Multi-Day / Swing View

Repeated P-shapes = sustained accumulation / short squeezes → higher timeframe uptrend likely.

A single P-shape in a sideways/weak higher timeframe = short-term bullish play only.

Always check weekly/monthly HVNs — if close, they act like magnets.

9. Example Trade (NIFTY-style case)

Assume today formed P-shape, POC = 24,950, VAH = 25,100, VAL = 24,900.

Tomorrow, if open at 25,020 (inside VA):

Price dips to 24,950 (POC), shows bullish engulfing on 5m chart.

Entry: Buy at 24,955.

Stop: 24,930 (25 points risk).

Target 1: 25,100 (145 pts reward).

Target 2: 25,250 (additional swing target).

Risk/Reward = ~1:5 if partials scaled.

10. Quick Checklist for P-shape Day After

✔ Mark POC, VAH, VAL, rejection tail LVN. ✔ Expect bullish bias → buy dips into POC/VAL. ✔ If opens above VAH → continuation play. ✔ If opens inside VA → dip-buy POC/VAL zone. ✔ If opens below VAL → wait for reclaim (bullish) or avoid (invalid). ✔ Confirmation = strong rejection candle + volume. ✔ Scale out partials, trail stops on remainder.

✨ Short Wrap

P-shape = bullish short-covering → next day bias up.

Trade plan = buy dips to POC/VAL or breakout above VAH with volume confirmation.

Invalidation = sustained break below VAL.

Best use = intraday continuation → can also hint at swing accumulation if repeated.

Double Distribution Profile

1. What is a Double Distribution Profile (DDP)?

Structure: Two clear “bellies” (two HVNs) separated by a thin area (LVN).

Psychology: Market first accepted one zone, then shifted strongly to another, creating a second acceptance area.

Bias: Neutral-to-directional, depending on which side of the LVN gets accepted the next day.

👉 Think of it like the market saying: “Old fair value was here, but now we’re also accepting trades up/down here.”

2. Key Characteristics

Two HVNs (value zones).

LVN in between = weak spot / decision zone.

If price stays in upper distribution → bullish.

If price falls back into lower distribution → bearish.

If it oscillates between both → range between HVNs.

3. Levels to Mark

Upper HVN (upper belly).

Lower HVN (lower belly).

LVN in between = battle zone.

Day High/Low.

Weekly POC/HVNs for confluence.

4. Next-Day Expectation Map

Bias depends on where price opens relative to LVN:

Above LVN, inside upper HVN → bullish continuation.

Below LVN, inside lower HVN → bearish continuation.

Inside LVN zone → chop early, breakout later.

5. Opening Scenarios & Trade Playbook

A — Open inside upper HVN

Market already accepted higher prices.

Plan:

Buy dips inside upper HVN.

Stop = below LVN.

Target = day high or next resistance above.

B — Open inside lower HVN

Market rejected higher zone, back to lower acceptance.

Plan:

Short rallies inside lower HVN.

Stop = above LVN.

Target = day low or next support below.

C — Open inside LVN (middle zone)

Tricky zone, expect volatility.

Plan:

If price accepts above LVN into upper HVN → go long.

If price rejects and falls into lower HVN → go short.

Don’t hold trades blindly in the middle.

6. Entry, Stop, Target Logic

Entry:

Buy dips in upper HVN.

Short rallies in lower HVN.

Trade LVN break/acceptance.

Stop:

Opposite side of LVN or HVN boundary.

Targets:

Opposite side of HVN.

Next daily/weekly node outside distribution.

7. Volume Confirmation

Strong acceptance = rising volume in HVN.

Weak breakout = low volume through LVN → likely false.

Valid breakout = strong volume surge through LVN with continuation.

8. Multi-Day / Swing View

Double distribution is often a trend day marker:

Day starts in one balance → then drives into another balance.

If next day also accepts new zone → trend continuation.

If not → price reverts back to original HVN.

9. Example Trade (NIFTY-style)

Suppose today’s DDP:

Lower HVN = 24,800–24,900

LVN = 24,950–24,970

Upper HVN = 25,020–25,120

Tomorrow open = 25,030 (inside upper HVN).

Plan: Buy dip into 25,020–25,040.

Stop = below 24,970 (LVN).

Target 1 = 25,100.

Target 2 = 25,250 (swing).

10. Quick Checklist for Double Distribution Day After

✔ Mark lower HVN, LVN, upper HVN. ✔ If open in upper HVN → bullish continuation. ✔ If open in lower HVN → bearish continuation. ✔ If open in LVN → wait for breakout → trade acceptance direction. ✔ Stops just outside LVN/HVN. ✔ Volume confirms acceptance vs rejection.

🔑 Wrap

Double Distribution = two acceptance zones + decision LVN.

Next day = bias comes from which HVN gets accepted.

Trade = fade edges inside HVNs, breakout at LVN.

Invalidation = failed acceptance (wick into LVN then reversal).

P-shape (Short-covering / Bullish)

Quick recap

Profile shows high volume at upper prices (bulge on top) — often created by shorts getting squeezed / buyers stepping in.

Bullish bias in next session(s) is likely.

What to draw before open

Prev day POC, VAH, VAL, HVN above POC, any tail/unused volume above (LVN below), daily high/low, weekly HVN, VWAP, short EMAs.

Expectation map

Base: upside continuation or at least support at upper HVN/POC.

Price may retest lower (POC) to gather liquidity, then continue up.

Opening scenarios & plans

A — Open above VAH

Bias: bullish.

Aggressive: buy on dip to POC/POC retest or on strong push above VAH with volume.

Conservative: wait for retest of POC/HVN turning into support.

B — Open inside VA

Bias: bullish lean.

Plan: look for price moving toward upper HVN/POC; buy retest of POC with rejection candle or sustained stay above POC.

C — Open below VAL

Bias: weakened — treat as possible short-term shakeout.

Plan: if price climbs quickly back into VA with good volume → buy the reclaim. If price stays below with selling volume, treat as failed P-shape.

Entries / Stops / Targets

Entries: long on retest + bullish PA at POC/HVN or breakout above VAH on volume.

Stops: below POC/HVN or recent swing low + buffer.

Targets: first target = short term resistance / prior high; second = measured move equal to width from LVN to HVN or next weekly HVN.

Volume confirmation

Upside moves should occur with strong buying volume. Short squeezes often show spikes in volume and price gaps.

Timeframes & tools

5m/15m for entries; check 1h/4h for where weekly HVN sits. Combine with RSI/ADX for momentum.

Swing / multi-day

Single P-day in a bull trend = continuation; repeated P shapes → strong accumulation and multi-day trend likely.

Common traps

Buying a P-shape without volume (false bullish).

Ignoring large TF resistance (weekly HVN) above.

Morning checklist

Mark POC/HVNs, VAH/VAL.

Is open above VAH? If yes, bias long but wait for retest/volume.

Use VWAP and POC as confluence for longs.

Journal prompts

Was upside move confirmed by volume? Where were shorts likely trapped?

Ambiguous bias until price chooses a side; the LVN between peaks is a decision zone.

Both HVNs + the LVN (the gap between them), POCs of each distribution (if using session split), VAH/VAL per distribution if available, higher TF HVNs.

Expectation map

Market may oscillate between the two HVNs (if both remain supported) OR resolve by breaking the LVN with volume → directional continuation to that side.

Opening scenarios & plans

A — Open above upper HVN

Bias: slightly bullish if acceptance continues above.

Trade: look for acceptance above the upper HVN; if price drops into LVN then rejects → long.

B — Open between the two HVNs (in the LVN)

Bias: key decision moment.

Plan: watch whether LVN acts as fast passage (quick move to either HVN) or as a magnet — trade the first clear directional breakout with volume confirmation. Avoid fading inside the LVN.

C — Open below lower HVN

Bias: bearish if price continues below lower HVN.

Trade: short on retest to lower HVN or POC.

Entries / Stops / Targets

Entries: trade the break of LVN with volume, or trade rejections at HVNs.

Stops: beyond the opposite HVN or beyond POC + buffer.

Targets: first target = the other HVN (if trading between peaks) or measured move beyond HVN equal to width of LVN (for breakout).

Volume confirmation

Breaks through LVN with expanding volume → high probability continuation.

If break happens on low volume, expect failure and reversion to the other HVN.

Timeframes & tools

5m/15m for intraday; check session-split profiles to see which peak formed earlier (time sequencing helps predict next move — older distribution vs newer).

Swing / multi-day

If double distributions persist across days → market is switching between two perceived fair values; wait for higher TF resolution.

Common traps

Jumping into LVN fades — LVNs often cause quick moves, so sizing must be smaller and stops tight.

Misreading which peak is dominant (recent HVN has more weight).

Morning checklist

Mark both HVNs and the LVN in between.

Note which HVN is the newer (more recent) peak.

Trade break of LVN only with volume; trade rejection at HVN with clear PA.

Journal prompts

Did the LVN break on volume or fail? Which HVN dominated and why?

Universal practical rules (apply to all shapes)

Always mark POC, VAH, VAL, HVNs & LVNs before the open.

Volume confirms — without volume, treat breakouts as low-probability.

Stops beyond nodes (give buffer). Nodes are not razor-thin lines; respect a few ticks.

Scale out: take partial profits at first HVN/POC and trail the rest.

Respect higher-TF bias: weekly/monthly HVNs can override day signals.

Position size to risk (use your 1% rule). Example recap (digit-by-digit):

Account ₹200,000 × risk 0.01 = ₹2,000.

Entry ₹1,000, stop ₹1,010 → risk/share = ₹10 → position = 200 shares.

D-Shape (Balanced Profile)Looks like “D” with fat middle

Market found fair value. Both buyers & sellers agreed. Range day.

- Previous day POC (center of D)- VAH & VAL (edges of balance)- HVN (middle zone)

- Market may stay range-bound until breakout- Expansion happens only if price accepts outside VAH/VAL

P-Shape (Short Covering / Bullish Bias)Fat top, thin bottom

Shorts got trapped → covered positions → bullish pressure.

- POC (upper half)- VAH (likely higher than usual)- LVN below profile (thin area)

- Bias is bullish if price holds above VAH/POC- If fails back below POC → weakness

- If above VAH/POC: Buy pullbacks to VAH/POC.- If back below POC: Short for mean reversion to VAL.

b-Shape (Long Liquidation / Bearish Bias)Fat bottom, thin top

Longs liquidated → selling pressure → bearish bias.

- POC (lower half)- VAL (very near POC)- LVN above profile

- Bias is bearish if price holds below POC- If reclaims POC → squeeze higher

- If below POC/VAL: Short on pullbacks to POC.- If above POC: Go long for mean reversion to VAH.

Double DistributionTwo fat bellies (two HVNs) with thin LVN in between

- If price accepts above LVN → target upper HVN.- If price rejects LVN → trade back to lower HVN.
