RIP and FLIP Setup

Introduction

Market loves to trap. Most traders jump into breakouts, thinking the move will continue, but many times price does the opposite. It rips through the level, traps traders, and then flips back in the other direction. That’s what I call the RIP and FLIP setup.

It’s nothing fancy — just a simple way to catch false breakouts. When others are stuck, we get the opportunity. This setup works in any market, any timeframe. Stocks, Nifty, crude, forex, even crypto — the logic is the same.

Instead of chasing, we wait for the trap. First the RIP (false breakout), then the FLIP (reversal). That’s where the real trade is.

🔹 What is RIP and FLIP?

The RIP and FLIP setup is a false breakout reversal strategy that helps traders identify when the market traps traders on one side (RIP), only to reverse direction and reward those who fade the breakout (FLIP).

RIP → The breakout happens, pulling in traders (they get “trapped”).

FLIP → The price reverses, flipping direction and invalidating the breakout.

This is essentially a swing failure setup — price breaks a key level, fails to sustain, and reverses back.

🔹 Why RIP and FLIP Works

Psychology of Breakouts

Retail traders chase breakouts expecting continuation.

Professionals fade false moves, capitalizing on liquidity.

Liquidity Grab

Market often pushes beyond obvious levels (highs/lows, trendlines, MAs) to grab stop-loss orders before reversing.

Mean Reversion

Price often reverts to the mean after an overextended breakout attempt.

🔹 Key Structure of RIP and FLIP

RIP (Trap / False Breakout)

Price moves beyond an important level (previous high, low, trendline, MA, indicator level).

This breakout excites traders to enter new positions in the direction of the breakout.

Pause / Small Move

A small continuation move happens (few candles in breakout direction).

This “confirms” the breakout for trapped traders, adding more positions.

FLIP (Reversal)

Price fails to sustain and moves back inside the breakout zone.

Stops of breakout traders get hit → momentum flips to the opposite side.

🔹 Bearish RIP and FLIP Example

Price breaks above resistance (RIP).

Small rally higher (traps breakout buyers).

Price reverses back below resistance (FLIP).

Trade Idea: Short entry after reversal confirmation, stop-loss above breakout high.

🔹 Bullish RIP and FLIP Example

Price breaks below support (RIP).

Small decline (traps breakout sellers).

Price reverses back above support (FLIP).

Trade Idea: Long entry after reversal confirmation, stop-loss below breakout low.

🔹 Rules for Trading RIP and FLIP

Identify an important level (previous high/low, trendline, MA, VWAP, indicator level).

Wait for a breakout beyond that level (the RIP).

Watch for a failure to sustain and a move back inside (the FLIP).

Confirm with:

Reversal candle pattern (engulfing, pin bar, doji).

Volume exhaustion / divergence.

Moving average or VWAP rejection.

Enter in opposite direction of breakout.

Place stop-loss beyond the breakout extreme.

🔹 Filters for Higher Probability

Trade in the direction of higher timeframe trend.

Use with moving averages / VWAP for confluence.

Avoid during low liquidity times (false signals more common).

🔹 Examples in NIFTY

Bullish Breakout Failure → Short Opportunity

Nifty breaks above previous swing high.

Fails to sustain, reverses lower.

Short trade with SL above breakout high.

Bearish Breakout Failure → Long Opportunity

Nifty breaks below swing low.

Traps sellers, reverses up.

Long trade with SL below breakout low.

With Moving Average Filter

Breakout occurs against MA trend → higher chance of failure.

Confirmation when price re-enters zone and aligns with MA.

RIP and FLIP is powerful because it identifies liquidity traps and lets you trade against weak hands.  It works in stocks, indices, commodities, and options trading (for directional bias).
