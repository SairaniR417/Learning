DELTA TO DHARMA

Capital Warfare: Risk & Position Sizing Battle Plan

Title: Protect the Base, Expand the Frontline Objective: Master risk per trade & position sizing so you stay in the game long enough to win big.

Opening Analogy — The Army General’s Secret

You’re the general. Your army is your capital.

If you send your entire army into the first battle and lose, the war is over.

If you send too few soldiers, you might miss the victory because you didn’t commit enough.

The art is in sending the right number — enough to win, but not enough to cripple you if it fails.

In trading: Every rupee you risk is a soldier. Risk management = deciding how many soldiers fight each battle.

1. Position Sizing — Your Ammo Control System

Method 1 — Fixed % Risk

Decide a fixed % of your account to risk per trade.

Example: Account ₹5,00,000, Risk 2% = ₹10,000 per trade.

Formula:

Position Size=Risk /Stop Loss Distance

Example: Stop-loss is ₹50 away → ₹10,000 ÷ ₹50 = 200 shares.

✅ Hack: Start with 1% risk until you have 50+ trades of consistent performance. Increase only if your drawdowns are small.

Method 2 — Volatility-Based (ATR Method)

Uses Average True Range to adapt to market volatility.

Formula:

Stop Loss Distance =ATR×Multiplier

Position Size=Risk /Stop Loss Distance ​

Example: Risk ₹10,000, ATR ₹25, Multiplier 2 → Stop ₹50 → 200 shares.

✅ Hack: In volatile markets, set multiplier higher (e.g., 2.5× ATR) to avoid being stopped out by noise.

Method 3 — Hybrid

Risk % decides the ₹ amount at risk,

ATR decides stop distance. This way your risk stays constant, and your stop adapts to market volatility.

2. Stop-Loss Placement — The Safety Fence

Technical Stop: Place beyond support/resistance, swing highs/lows, trendlines.

Volatility Stop: Place beyond ATR-based buffer.

Time Stop: Exit after X bars if trade stagnates.

Example: If stock is at ₹1,000, support is at ₹980, ATR = ₹10:

Technical Stop: ₹978 (just below support)

ATR Stop (2×): ₹980 - ₹20 = ₹960

✅ Hack: Never place stops exactly at obvious levels (e.g., ₹1,000 or ₹980). Smart money hunts liquidity there. Hide it a little deeper.

3. R-Multiples & Expectancy — Your Scoreboard

R = Risk Unit.

If risk ₹10,000 = 1R.

Profit ₹20,000 = +2R, Loss ₹5,000 = -0.5R.

Expectancy: E=(Win%×Avg Win)−(Loss%×Avg Loss)

Example: Win rate = 40%, Avg Win = 2R, Loss rate = 60%, Avg Loss = 1R:E=(0.4×2)−(0.6×1)=0.8−0.6=0.2R

✅ Hack: Track trades in R, not ₹, to remove emotional bias from P&L.

4. The 2% Rule — Don’t Worship It

Rule says: “Don’t risk more than 2% of capital per trade.”

Truth: 2% is an upper limit, not a default setting.

For new traders: start with 0.5–1% risk per trade.

Increase gradually only if:

Drawdowns stay <10%.

You have 100+ trades with stable expectancy.

5. Adjusting for Market Conditions

Volatile Market: Reduce position size → price moves are bigger, so less size keeps risk constant.

Calm Market: You can slightly increase size → stops are tighter, so exposure is smaller.

Example:

Calm day ATR = ₹5, Risk ₹10,000 → 2,000 shares.

Volatile day ATR = ₹20, Risk ₹10,000 → 500 shares.

Practical Tips, Hacks, and Tricks

Hacks:

Stop-loss Cushion: Add 0.2–0.5× ATR beyond your technical stop to avoid stop hunting.

Half-Size Entry: If unsure about volatility, start with 50% position and add later if trade confirms.

Kill Switch Rule: If you lose 3R in a day, stop trading — market is not aligned with your strategy today.

ATR Alert: Use alerts when ATR spikes → indicates regime change, adjust position sizing.

Mindset Tips:

Think like a casino: You decide bet size, not the game.

Surviving is winning — most blow-ups happen from over-leveraging, not bad setups.

Capital is like oxygen — you don’t think about it until it’s running out.
