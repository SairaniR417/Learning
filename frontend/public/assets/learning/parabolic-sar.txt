The Parabolic SAR

1. Introduction to Parabolic SAR

Full form: Parabolic Stop and Reverse.

Inventor: J. Welles Wilder (also creator of RSI, ATR, ADX).

Core idea: Price always trends until it stops and reverses. PSAR helps identify this shift.

Representation: Dots above or below candles.

Dots below candles → Uptrend.

Dots above candles → Downtrend.

2. Formula of Parabolic SAR

The formula is recursive, meaning today’s value is built from yesterday’s.

For Uptrend:

SARtoday=SARyesterday+AF×(EP−SARyesterday)SAR_{today} = SAR_{yesterday} + AF \times (EP - SAR_{yesterday})

For Downtrend:

SARtoday=SARyesterday−AF×(SARyesterday−EP)SAR_{today} = SAR_{yesterday} - AF \times (SAR_{yesterday} - EP)

SAR = Stop and Reverse value.

EP (Extreme Point) = Highest high (uptrend) or lowest low (downtrend).

AF (Acceleration Factor) = Sensitivity multiplier. Default = 0.02, max = 0.20.

3. Parameters & Settings

Step (AF): How quickly SAR reacts.

Smaller (0.01–0.02): smoother, fewer signals.

Larger (0.05–0.1): faster, more signals but more noise.

Max AF: Caps acceleration, default 0.20.

4. Interpretation

Dots below price = bullish continuation until broken.

Dots above price = bearish continuation until broken.

Crossover: When SAR flips side → possible trend reversal.

Spacing:

Dots close to candles → trend losing steam.

Dots far → strong momentum.

5. Types of Usage

🔹 A. Trend Following

Enter long when SAR flips below candles.

Exit or short when SAR flips above candles.

🔹 B. Trailing Stop-Loss Tool

Traders use SAR dots as dynamic SL.

Example: In long trade → shift SL to PSAR dot of last candle.

🔹 C. Momentum Confirmation

Combine with ADX/EMA to confirm trend strength.

Avoid using SAR in choppy sideways markets.

6. Trading Strategies with PSAR

1. Parabolic SAR + Moving Averages

Rule:

Long when price > 50 EMA and SAR flips below.

Short when price < 50 EMA and SAR flips above.

2. Parabolic SAR + RSI

Buy when RSI > 50 and SAR below.

Sell when RSI < 50 and SAR above.

Avoid false flips in overbought/oversold zones.

3. Parabolic SAR + ADX

Use ADX > 20 to filter strong trends.

SAR signals are valid only when ADX confirms trend strength.

4. Parabolic SAR Scalping (Intraday)

5-min or 15-min charts.

Enter at SAR flip with VWAP confirmation.

Exit at opposite SAR or fixed RR ratio.

7. Advanced Techniques

Dynamic AF: Adjust AF based on volatility (use ATR).

Multi-timeframe SAR:

Use higher TF SAR for direction (1h), lower TF for entry (5min).

SAR Zones: If SAR flips back & forth in 2-3 bars → avoid trade (sideways filter).

8. Strengths & Weaknesses

✅ Strengths:

Simple, visual, no clutter.

Great as trailing stop tool.

Works best in strong trends.

❌ Weaknesses:

Whipsaws in range/choppy markets.

Cannot predict sideways movement.

Too sensitive if settings aren’t optimized.

9. Trader Psychology with SAR

Beginner’s trap: Taking every SAR flip → chopped in sideways market.

Pro mindset: Use SAR as confirmation + SL tool, not standalone.

Discipline: Must accept SAR flip as “end of trend” even if ego says otherwise.

10. Tips & Pro Tricks

Use default (0.02, 0.20) for swing trading, but 0.01, 0.10 for intraday.

Always pair with a trend filter (EMA, Supertrend, ADX).

Best applied in commodities, forex, indices where trends stretch.

Avoid in low-volume, sideways equities.

11. Example (NIFTY)

NIFTY trending from 22,000 → 22,800.

PSAR flips below candles at 22,100 → Buy signal.

Trail SL on each PSAR dot → exit at 22,760 when SAR flips above.

Captured ~650 points risk-managed move.

12. Final Verdict

PSAR is not a predictor, it’s a reactionary tool.

Alone it gives whipsaws, but with filters, it becomes a deadly combo.

Best role = Trailing Stop Engine in a trend-following system.
