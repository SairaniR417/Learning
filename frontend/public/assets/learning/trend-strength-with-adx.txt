The ADX (Average Directional Index)

Chapter 1: The Foundations of ADX

1.1 What is ADX?

ADX = Average Directional Index, developed by Welles Wilder (the same genius who gave us RSI, ATR, and Parabolic SAR).

Purpose: Measure trend strength, not direction.

Range: 0 to 100.

0–20 → Weak / No Trend (choppy).

20–25 → Emerging Trend.

25–50 → Strong Trend.

50–75 → Very Strong Trend.

75–100 → Extreme Trend (rare, often unsustainable).

ADX does not tell you bullish or bearish — only strength. That’s why it’s paired with +DI (Directional Indicator Plus) and –DI (Directional Indicator Minus).

Chapter 2: The ADX Formula (Under the Hood)

Calculate True Range (TR).

Find +DM (Directional Movement Plus) and –DM (Directional Movement Minus):

+DM = today’s high – yesterday’s high (if > yesterday’s low – today’s low).

–DM = yesterday’s low – today’s low (if > today’s high – yesterday’s high).

Smooth them using Wilder’s smoothing method.

Compute +DI = (Smoothed +DM / Smoothed TR) × 100.

Compute –DI = (Smoothed –DM / Smoothed TR) × 100.

DX = (|+DI – –DI| / (+DI + –DI)) × 100.

ADX = Smoothed DX over a chosen period (default: 14).

In short → ADX = smoothed average of how far +DI and –DI diverge.

Chapter 3: Reading ADX

3.1 The Classic Levels

Below 20: Ranging / No trend. Avoid trend trades.

20–25: Trend beginning. A watch zone.

25–50: Trend healthy & tradable.

50–75: Very strong trend, but caution (late stage).

Above 75: Exhaustion possible, sharp reversals ahead.

3.2 +DI and –DI Crossovers

+DI crosses above –DI → Bulls in control.

–DI crosses above +DI → Bears in control.

ADX rising above 25 confirms trend strength behind the crossover.

Chapter 4: ADX Trading Strategies

4.1 Trend-Following with ADX

Buy when +DI > –DI and ADX > 25.

Sell when –DI > +DI and ADX > 25.

Exit when ADX turns down (trend losing steam).

4.2 ADX Pullback Strategy

In a strong uptrend (ADX > 25, +DI dominant), enter on pullbacks near support.

In a strong downtrend (ADX > 25, –DI dominant), short on rallies near resistance.

4.3 ADX with Moving Averages

Filter trades:

Only buy MA crossovers when ADX > 25.

Avoid false signals when ADX < 20.

4.4 ADX Breakout Strategy

If ADX < 20 for a long period → market is “coiling.”

Breakout from support/resistance with ADX rising = high probability trade.

Chapter 5: ADX Psychology

Ranging market (ADX < 20): Traders get trapped in false breakouts.

ADX rising: Herd behavior, trend following builds momentum.

ADX falling after a peak: Traders booking profits, new participants hesitate, volatility drops.

Extreme ADX (70+): Euphoria/panic — often near the end.

Chapter 6: Advanced ADX Techniques

6.1 Multi-Timeframe ADX

Weekly ADX > 25 confirms long-term trend.

Daily ADX > 25 gives entry signals within that trend.

Intraday ADX helps refine entries.

6.2 ADX + ATR (Trend Strength + Volatility)

ADX = strength, ATR = volatility.

Together → identify strong + volatile = explosive trades.

Strong ADX but low ATR = slow grind.

High ATR but low ADX = whipsaws.

6.3 ADX Divergences

Price making higher highs, but ADX making lower highs → trend weakening.

Price making lower lows, ADX making lower highs → bearish trend losing steam.

6.4 ADX with Volume Profile

ADX rising with volume confirmation = strong institutional activity.

ADX rising without volume = weak hands driving trend → riskier.

Chapter 7: Common Mistakes with ADX

Using ADX alone → it needs DI lines or other tools.

Entering when ADX is already above 50 → often too late.

Ignoring timeframes → ADX can be strong on 5-min chart but meaningless on weekly.

Forgetting that ADX lags (it smooths data).

Chapter 8: Tips & Best Practices

Use 25-level as universal “trend filter.”

Combine with candlestick price action for entries/exits.

In intraday, ADX 5–10 period works better (faster). For swing, 14–20 period is ideal.

Always check ADX slope: rising = strengthening, falling = weakening.

Chapter 9: ADX in Different Market Types

Stocks: Great for trend filters in positional trades.

Forex: Very useful due to long trending phases.

Commodities: ADX works well since trends are sharp.

Options: Use ADX to decide whether to buy options (when trend is strong) or sell options (when ADX < 20, range-bound).

Chapter 10: The ADX Holy Trinity

ADX > 25 → Trade only trends.

+DI/–DI crossover → Direction.

Candles/Patterns → Entry & exit refinement.

Final Thought: ADX is not about predicting — it’s about aligning with the existing force. It tells you when to attack with size and when to stay flat. Think of it as a trend radar: the higher it goes, the stronger the winds.
